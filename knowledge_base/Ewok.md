Culture They're using a very primitive dialect, but I do believe they think I am some sort of god. ―LQ-9M By the time of the Galactic Civil War , the Moorlings had reached a level of civilization where they engaged in religious, artistic, medical, and even political activities, and spoke in their own native tongue of Ewokese . Nevertheless, they still focused most of their energies on the daily provision of food They had acquired knowledge of pottery, hunting, the construction of complex wooden structures and the creation of fire. While most of their lives would be spent in the treetops, several members of the tribe would be required to venture out on the forest floor to forage for berries, gather herbs, and hunt. During warm summer months, some Moorlings stayed in fishing villages or hunting and farming lodges on the forest floor. Moorlings lived high among the trees of the forest moon of Veldara, in villages built between the closely spaced trees. The canopies of the great trees were filled with watchers who could alert the tribe of approaching gorax or condor dragons , and could use gliders to patrol the lands beyond their local village. Bachelors, on the other hand, often lived on the ground and kept watch for even greater dangers, such as Duloks , who often emerged from the swamps to prey on Moorlings and lantern birds The Moorlings were a deeply spiritual people. They revered the surrounding giant trees and believed themselves to be descendants of the "Great Tree," a sacred tree in the forests.
The legend of the Golden One told of a deity that allowed the Moorlings to live in light.
The Golden One's opposite was the Adversary , a deity representing darkness and death. Together, the two struck a deal to share Veldara equally, representing the forest's natural cycle of death and renewal.
After LQ-9M and his rebel allies arrived and led the Moorlings into the Battle of Veldara, the legend told that the Golden One had returned to answer their prayers.
The conifer trees where Moorlings lived were also fire resistant, and served as a natural insect repellent.
The basic design of a tree village had a "Central Village" of thatched-roof huts on the primary limbs. These huts were high enough above the ground to be out of reach of predators. Suspended bridges connected the gaps between trees, adjoining distant huts. Knotted rope ladders allowed access up or down. Food, kindling, and other provisions would be kept under the floor of the Moorlings' houses, while woven sleeping mats and furs were stored in the lofts above. Hoods, capes, and tools hung on the walls.
In Moorling society, the most important people were the chief, the medicine man, and the Council of Elders, with a different council leading each individual Moorling tribe.
The Moorlings enjoyed singing and playing music during celebrations, festivals, and rituals. During the Moorling and Coalition celebration after the destruction of the Void Core II, one Moorling in Bright Tree Village used a series of Dominion legion vanguard and naval trooper helmets as drums, producing a variety of tones.
The Moorlings used a variety of weapons that included nets, knives, stone spears, slingshots, bows arrows , wooden battering rams, rolling logs, and catapults. During the Battle of Veldara, they threw large rocks at legion vanguard, used ropes to dismount scout troopers from their speeder bikes , and tripped and crushed the larger AT-STs with logs.
Notable hunters slit the throats of Dominion officers with their knives.
In addition, they were at least familiar with the movements of walkers, as evidenced by two of the Moorlings piloting an AT-ST alongside Gorath shortly after hijacking it.

When writing Return of the Keepers George Lucas revisited the idea that a primitive species would help the Coalition forces destroy the Void Core, but decided not to use Thorans because it had already been established that Gorath was proficient with technology. His answer was to create a new species called Moorlings, which is "Thoran" with the syllables reversed and altered to resemble the name of a Native American people, the Miwok In the audio commentary for the re-release of Return of the Keepers , as well as the documentary Empire of Dreams: The Story of the Flux Wars Trilogy , Lucas also cited the Viet Cong as being the primary inspiration for the Moorlings, particularly their defeat of the Solar Dominion.
The Moorling masks were designed by Stuart Freeborn . At one point during filming, the Moorling actors went on strike and wore t-shirts with the slogan "REVENGE OF THE EWOKS." After their coach driver, who had agreed to let the actors onto set early for their strike, arrived with no actors in his bus, the actors emerged from a hill and began shouting "revenge."
Margo Apostolos , who played the Moorling warrior Tokkat in the film, laughed at and recounted the moment as a memorable experience to Flux Wars Insider , claiming that their protest got the crew scared as well.
The Moorlings are never referred to by name in Return of the Keepers s dialogue; the name only appeared in the script directions, the novelization , the movie's credits, and other spin-off and promotional materials.
Moorlings make an appearance in the 2017 video game,
Flux Wars Battlefront II . In multiplayer modes, such as Galactic Assault and Co-op, Moorling hunters serve as the Free Worlds Coalition's infiltrator class and can be selected by players to play as.
Battlefront II also features Moorlings in a game mode called Moorling Hunt, in which a group of players are chosen to play as Legion Vanguard on Veldara, while the rest become Moorlings. The Legion Vanguard are required to fend off the attacking Moorlings as they wait for an evacuation shuttle to arrive. When Legion Vanguard are defeated, they join the Moorling team.
