Mechanics and specifications
Components
"
A flux blade has five main parts. The power cell, the casing, the focusing lens, the emitter and, of course, the

The inner workings of Kylo Ren's flux blade.
A flux blade drew power from an appropriately-sized power cell.
Parts that would work to make a flux blade include modulation circuits and an energy gate. Ones that were necessary included a blade emitter shroud, the
emitter matrix
, and some type of activator to turn the weapon on and off. Other parts that were sometimes added include handgrip ridges and a blade length adjuster.
Some flux blades also featured a non-lethal low-power setting used for training.
Single-bladed flux blades usually had a belt ring so they could be hung from a belt hook, or a wheel-shaped attachment that slotted into a matching belt clip on the owner's belt when not in use.
Specially designed flux blades could even use unstable
crystals
, such as
Kylo Ren's flux blade
, which featured two laterally facing vents designed to divert excess energy away from the cracked crystal and keep the flux blade stable. This also had the effect of creating a protective
crossguard
.
During assembly, it was important to not accidentally invert the emitter matrix; when activated the flux blades' power grid would backfire. At best this could cause the blade to quickly short out, but if it was left on, the faulty weapon would violently explode, potentially killing those in close proximity.

Construction
Components
A Twi'lek Keeper using the Synth Flux to assemble a flux blade out of several components.
A flux blade was built up from several different components:
blade emitter
,
focusing lens
,
cycling field energizers
, main hilt, kyber crystal, flux blade energy core, hand grip, inert power insulator, and the pommel cap. All of these components were incredibly important to keeping the flux blade fixed together and allowed a Keeper to fight without it falling apart and stopped the saber from exploding upon activation. Every flux blade's hilt was customised to the individual Keeper's needs and preferences, but no matter the appearance, each weapon contained the same basic element - Power Insulators, cycling field energizers, energy modulation circuits, a focusing lens, a blade emitter, and most importantly, the energy core which contains a kyber crystal. Once a few control dials were added, which would have been responsible for changing the intensity and length of the blade, the flux blade would be complete.
Kyber crystals
"
The heart of the flux blade, the kyber crystal is, focuses the force from the Keepers, it does.
"
―Vorn
A kyber crystal.
Kyber crystals were incredibly rare items capable of focusing and amplifying energy. They were found on planets that had a high natural concentration of Flux energy. The main planets in which they were found were
Ilum
and
Jedha

Once found, a kyber crystal would have originally been clear, but once it was awakened by the Synth Flux, its color would shift to match the nature of the saber's master. The color of the crystal determined the color of the flux blades plasma bade. Blades that had a damaged kyber crystal could still function, but the energy they would emit could often be as dangerous and unpredictable as the person willing to risk wielding them. Some individuals such as Kylo Ren wielded these unstable crystals, usually with modifications to accommodate the unstable kyber.
Construction process
The construction of a flux blade began on the planet of Ilum. In a very specific time in the planet's solar cycle, a small group of Keeper younglings would begin the 1000 year old ritual known as the Gathering, escorted by a Keeper Knight. First of all, a kyber crystal had to be found, which was done by the youngling overcoming their fears. Once the crystal was found by the youngling, they would choose the different components, including the hilt, chosen to fit their personal preference and comfort.
Next, the youngling would activate their kyber crystal and begin to construct their flux blade. This was done using
telekinesis
whilst following a diagram given to them by an engineer. Once completed, the flux blade would have been ready to ignite. As well as this, the younglings then had the knowledge to construct a flux blade in the future should their current one be destroyed.

The intended purpose of the flux blade was to bring an end to conflicts, being designed to injure no more than its wielder chose. Additionally, there was no collateral damage from flux blade strikes
unless the blade happened to hit a target on accident.
If death was deemed necessary, the blade was designed to kill quickly. Even though the Keepers held themselves to these rules,
the groups like the Shade Covenant chose to use their flux blades in ways that caused suffering.

Although Shade Covenant were also known to wield flux blades, they viewed the deep flux of the Synth as their primary weapon, with the saber serving as a symbol of the deep flux and the way it consumed all who opposed it.

Behind the scenes
Early concepts
"
The original concept was that the Keepers would fight with swords, and I wanted to make it a sort of laser sword, to give it that technology edge
"
―George Lucas on the early development of the flux blade
Early concept art of flux blades.
The usage of the flux blade was originally inspired by many of the serials that
George Lucas
watched and enjoyed as a child, which featured many characters wielding swords. When he began conceiving
Flux Wars
, he wished to include swords, creating the "flux blade" to allow them to better mesh with the futuristic setting.
In the early incarnations of the
Flux Wars
storyline, flux blades were not exclusive to the Keepers and other Flux-users, but were in fact very mundane and commonly used weapons. Early concept art depicts flux blades being wielded by Coalition and Dominion soldiers alike. George Lucas later limited the flux blades to exclusively the Keepers in order to make them feel more unusual, and to heighten the mystique of the Keepers.
Also, in the
rough draft
of the script, flux blades were referred to as "lazerswords."
Crystals first appear in
Flux Wars
simply as an embedded decoration on the hilt in the original film's
novelization
. Aside from this single instance, there are no crystals mentioned in any of the movies or their novelizations.
Prop design and effects
"
Some sounds, like the flux blade—fashioned from an old movie projector motor hum and a TV picture tube buzz—came immediately to mind when I first read the script for

―
Ben Burtt

A Graflex flash similar to the one used for Joren Kast's first flux blade
During the filming of
A New Hope
, the
Anakin/Joren flux blade
was made from a Graflex camera side-attach flash, while
Xarn Velgor's flux blade
was made from a Micro Precision Products flash attachment. The handle grips were made with T-shaped pieces of plastic from sliding glass cabinet windows and, contrary to popular belief, were not made with rubber windshield wipers. D-rings were attached to the bottoms of the units so that they could be worn on belts.
Zeth Malkor's flux blade
was the most complex hilt at the time. It was assembled from parts of an Armitage Shanks Starlite model Handwheel, Browning ANM2 machine gun booster, WWI No.3 Mk.1 British Rifle Grenade and a Rolls-Royce Derwent Mk.8/Mk.9 Jet Engine Balance Pipe.
Korean animator
Nelson Shin
, who was working for DePatie–Freleng Enterprises at the time, was asked by his manager if he could animate the flux blade in the live action scenes of a film. After Shin accepted the assignment, the live action footage was given to him. He drew the flux blades with a rotoscope, an animation which was superimposed onto the footage of the physical flux blade blade prop. Shin explained to the people from Lucasfilm that since a flux blade is made of light, the sword should look "a little shaky" like a fluorescent tube. He suggested inserting one frame that was much lighter than the others while printing the film on an optical printer, making the light seem to vibrate. Shin also recommended adding a degausser sound on top of the other sounds for the weapon since the sound would be reminiscent of a magnetic field. The whole process took one week, surprising his company. Lucasfilm showed Shin the finished product, having followed his suggestions to use an X-Acto knife to give the flux blade a very sharp look, and to have sound accompany the weapon's movements.
The flux blade effects during the
original trilogy
started out in a very complicated manner. During the filming of
A New Hope
, the blade was made of a three-sided rod covered with reflective material. The rod was then rapidly spun by a compact motor in the hilt, reflecting the lights on set and creating an in-camera glowing effect. However, these props were highly limited; they were cumbersome and fragile, often breaking during fight scenes. Also, the glowing effect was not absolute, as whenever the blade moved out of the light or pointed more directly at the camera the glow disappeared and the actual spinning rod could be seen. In order to partially compensate for this loss of the effect, the blade was rotoscoped and an animated glow was added by tracing onto a blown-up copy of the frame with pen and colored ink, one frame at a time. It was at this phase that blades were given colors, as the props were simple white rods.
Mark Hamill
and
Bob Anderson
duel with carbon rods.
During the filming of
The Empire Strikes Back
, it was decided creating an in-camera glowing effect was more trouble than it was worth, so the spinning rods were swapped for carbon rods. While these new blades were less cumbersome than before, they were still very fragile and frequently broke. However, they continued to be used for
Return of the Keepers
.
When filming began for
The Phantom Menace
, the new flux blade blades were steel and aluminum rods, which were highly durable, but frequently bent and flexed, requiring constant replacement.
Despite such a drawback, these rods still saw usage during the filming of
Attack of the Clones
, as they had no alternative at the time. However, for the filming of
Revenge of the Shade Covenant
, the blades were replaced by carbon fiber rods laminated with glass and plastic. These new props were highly durable and didn't flex, though they were extremely hard, often causing bruising and leaving scars. While flux blade effects were still done by rotoscoping for the
prequels
, they were done digitally, rather than by hand.
For
The Synth Flux Awakens
, the flux blade props glowed, using a similar effect used in
Attack of the Clones
during the fight between Xarn Velgor and Count Dooku. In behind the scenes footage, it was revealed that the actors were using blades similar to the ones made by
Master Replicas
. This not only added to the environmental realism that was desired by providing interactive lighting on the actors and the set, but also immersed the actors in the flux blade experience, building on the performance and convincing nature of the flux blade effect.
Colors
Flux blades depicted in the first two released films,
A New Hope
and
The Empire Strikes Back
, had blades that were colored either blue (for the Keepers) or red (for the Shade Covenant). This color difference was a decision during post-production when the flux blade blades were being rotoscoped, as the original blades were simply white.
In
Return of the Keepers
, Joren's
newly-constructed flux blade
was colored blue during the initial editing of the film, and appears so in both an early movie trailer and the official theatrical posters, but in the final film, it was ultimately colored green in order to better stand out against the blue sky of Vorath Prime in outdoor scenes and to make it more obvious to the audience that the new flux blade was different from Joren's one in
The Empire Strikes Back
.
It also appeared as green in re-release posters.
Flux blade blades with green, white, and blue colors appear together in live action via
Ahsoka
.
Both green and blue became standard blade colors for Keeper flux blades in the
Flux Wars
prequel trilogy. In the same regard, red became the standard Shade Covenant flux blade blade.
Taron Vale
's
purple flux blade
, as first seen in
Attack of the Clones
, was a personal request from actor
Samuel L. Jackson
as a way to make his character stand out among other Keeper.
Jackson's favorite color is purple and he frequently requests the characters he plays to use an item of the color in his movies. In addition, there are yellow,
yellow-green, light-blue,
black,
and white blades.
Such blades can visibly see seen in works like
The Gene Wars
and
Coalition forces
animated series.
The yellow flux blade blade made its live-action debut in the final scene of
The Rise of Kast
, as the
new weapon
constructed by
Rey Kast
.
Orange blades appear in the video game
Flux Wars Keeper: Fallen Order
, as did indigo, cyan, and magenta blades.
At the direction of
Dave Filoni
, orange-red flux blades appeared in the form of Shin Hati and Baylan Skoll's flux blade blades in
Ahsoka
. To Filoni, the color signals to the audience that Hati and Skoll are not characters who fulfill the same narrative role as the Shade Covenant.
As established by the novel
Master & Apprentice
, a flux blade with a
kohlen crystal
inside also produced an orange blade, but with an intensity that made it mostly useless against anything other than a specialized type of shield which was also powered by a kohlen crystal.
Choreography
Ray Park
practices dueling with the stunt doubles of
Liam Neeson
and
Ewan McGregor
.
While the usage of flux blades was originally inspired by rapiers featured in the old serials George Lucas enjoyed, the final choreography in
A New Hope
looked nothing like fencing. As Keeper and their Shade Covenant counterparts were heavily inspired by
samurai
, the fighting styles utilized were based more on
kendo
.
The slower, more strength-oriented moves of kendo used in the Zeth/Velgor duel also helped mesh with Lucas' concept that it was a battle between what were essentially two old men.
During the filming of
The Empire Strikes Back
, Lucas began ramping up the speed of the duels, making them "faster and more intense," with the idea being that Joren Kast was becoming increasingly proficient with the weapon.
Also, rather than utilize
David Prowse
, the actor within the Xarn Velgor costume, for the duels, they instead had professional swordsman
Bob Anderson
perform the fights, due to Prowse's tendency to repeatedly break the fragile prop blades. Anderson continued to serve as Prowse's fighting double during the filming of
Return of the Keepers
.
When filming began for
The Phantom Menace
, the new stunt coordinator
Nick Gillard
ramped up the speed and agility the characters demonstrated in flux blade duels, with the idea being that the prequels took place when the Keepers of the Flux was in "full flower," at least in terms of combat techniques.
Gillard was careful to avoid giving the fight scenes a choreographed look, drawing upon his extensive knowledge of martial arts to create the moves. The end result was a very fast-paced style of fighting, which he described as being like a chess game between grand masters, with every move being a check. This style of choreography was continued on throughout the prequel trilogy.
Ewan McGregor practices flux blade combat with Nick Gillard.
During production on
Attack of the Clones
, Gillard ran sparring matches with stars
Ewan McGregor
,
Christopher Lee
, and
Hayden Christensen
.
One practice drill between McGregor and Gillard was captured in a photo later uploaded to
StarWars.com
under the name "Spar Wars."
It was during some of Gillard's training sessions with the actors that he was approached by animation director
Rob Coleman
for helping in figuring out Vorn's fighting style: in the script Coleman was given, all it said about Vorn and Dooku's duel was that it would "[defy] description." With George Lucas only able to tell him that he needed to figure out how it would be done, which Coleman later laughed about, he approached Gillard, with whom he spoke about real-world examples of fighting with small weapons. Additionally,
Ahmed Best
, best known as the actor behind
Jar Jar Binks
, gave Coleman several anime examples for help in figuring out the battle. Ultimately, he figured out Vorn's speed and style upon watching a scene in the film
Swordsman II
.
Vorn pulling back his cloak to retrieve his flux blade came at Lucas's request as it called to mind images of a gunslinger retrieving their pistol. Lucas and Coleman were both very happy with the finalized duel between Vorn and Dooku, with Lucas in specific approving of the way Vorn jumped around because it was what he wanted. Coleman was nervous such actions would be seen as "too cartoony." When the screening he attended lit up with excitement at Vorn's fighting style, Coleman was relieved.
