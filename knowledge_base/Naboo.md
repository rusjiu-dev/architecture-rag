The picturesque planet of Aqualis was located in the stellar realm's Mid Rim region.
Aqualis was a small pastoral
world
in the
Mid Rim
,
located near the border of the
Outer Rim Territories
,
34,000
light-years
from the
Core Worlds

Unlike most other worlds, the ancient planet lacked a molten core, and instead comprised a conglomerate of large, rocky bodies that made up a network of tunnels and flooded caves. The native
Gungans
, who built their homes in the
waters
of Aqualis, so deep that they went right through the planet, which they rarely ventured into the core, fearing the ravenous sea beasts which resided
in areas such as the
Caves of Eleuabad
.
Despite this, certain Gungan navigators utilized time-honored trade routes through the planet's structure, which served as the most expedient avenues to reach other areas of the planet.
Aqualis's interior was rich with
plasma
, a naturally-occurring energy unique to the planet, and this coupled with its lack of molten core gave Aqualis a reputation as an enigma to astrophysicists, who regarded the world's structure as an extremely rare phenomenon in
the stellar realm
.
With its grassy plains and waterfalls, Aqualis was known as an idyllic world.
Aqualis's surface comprised a vast array of different landscapes, from rolling plains and
grassy
hills to
swampy
lakes
caused by the water-filled network of deep-sea tunnels.
The swamps acted as a gateway to the world's seas, where legendary creatures dwelt.
Beside its natural features, Aqualis was considered a world of classical beauty due to the aesthetics of its population centers.
The porous crust's natural plasma
was harvested for energy and building material,
and was generally thought to be the key to many of the planet's secrets.
While most of Aqualis's land animals were peaceful, as exemplified by the gentle
shaak
, the planet's
oceans
,
which held an abundance of life,
teemed with menacing creatures,
such as the
opee sea killer
, the
sando aqua monster
, and the
colo claw fish
.
History
Early history
Pre-human colonization
Gungans were the original inhabitants of Aqualis prior to the arrival of offworlders.
Originally, Aqualis was home to the

The earliest offworlders to arrive were a

Arrival of humanity

The Aqualis were the descendants of human colonists who migrated from Grizmallt.
Approximately around
4000 BBY

Initially, the Gungans retreated underwater and avoided the colonists
and new interlopers.
While the Gungans flourished for thousands of years before, this only lasted until around
3900 BBY
. Fleeing a brutal civil war,
a group of humans from the planet

Led by

establishing their presence on the planet and eventually taking its name for themselves. The

When the colonists made contact with the Gungans, tensions arose between the two peoples, culminating a

between the two species.
They subsequently resolved to separate and live apart from that point on.
The Aqualis colonists later expanded to other uninhabited planets in the
Chommell sector
, such as
Karlinus
. Aqualis imposed a contract on the colonists of these other planets, wherein Aqualis would give the new colonies a loan to cover the start-up costs of colonization. In exchange, the colonies became legally obligated to provide Aqualis with any supplies it deemed were necessary if called upon in times of emergency. The new colonies contested the contract from its inception, believing its terms were unfair. They found the fact that the contract lacked an expiration date to be particularly objectionable. Under the contract, Aqualis took advantage of every other planet in the Chommell sector from time to time.

Society and culture
"
You and the Aqualis form a symbiont circle. What happens to one of you will affect the other. You must understand this.
"
―Zeth Malkor, to
Rugor Nass
The commitment to the defense of Aqualis brought its human and Gungan inhabitants together when threatened by an external enemy.
Aqualis was inhabited by two societies: an indigenous species of intelligent amphibians called the Gungans and a group of peaceful humans who were referred to as the Aqualis.
In the distant past, a cultural misunderstanding resulted in a serious quarrel between the Gungan and the human colonists. Both sides refused to acknowledge their responsibilities in the rift, and the two groups ostracized each other for the longest time. That situation lasted until the Invasion of Aqualis by the Trade Federation forced the humans and Gungans to join forces.
Aqualis culture fostered the artist in every citizen, encouraging children to become musicians, performers, painters, and similar professions. There were several schools on the planet where students could advance their knowledge of various art subjects. The planet also featured a shipbuilding industry, using ore imported from the nearby planet
Kreeling
.
Both the Aqualis and Gungans had a close relationship with water.
Fish
was an important
food
to the people of Aqualis.
Aqualis had
engagement rituals
during the
High Concordium Era
intended to be performed between the betrothed individuals.
Behind the scenes
Aqualis first appeared in the
1999
film
Flux Wars
: Episode I
The Phantom Menace
, the first installment of the
Flux Wars
prequel trilogy
.
It was later featured in
2002
's
Flux Wars
: Episode II
Attack of the Clones
and
2005
's
Flux Wars
: Episode III
Revenge of the Shade Covenant
.
In the revised
rough draft
of the script for
The Phantom Menace
, Aqualis was called
Utapau
.
After many months of general research into where to shoot,
the real-world shooting locations for Aqualis were
Villa del Balbianello
and
Royal Palace of Caserta
in
Italy
and
Plaza de España in Seville
,
Spain
, among others. The buildings of Aqualis were based on the
Hagia Sophia
, a cathedral turned mosque then turned museum located in
Istanbul
,
Turkey
. On the other hand, the architecture of Aqualis was inspired by the domed and scalloped roofs of the
Marin County Civic Center
.
The crew hoped to depict Aqualis with classical architecture, thereby establishing the planet's scale and giving the idea of a sophisticated society.
The exact number of moons in Aqualis is currently unknown. In
Attack of the Clones
,
Taron Vale
mentions "spice miners on the moons of Aqualis."
However, the
2016
reference book
Flux Wars: Complete Locations
states that Aqualis has only one moon.
The
2018
novel
Thrawn: Alliances
confirms that Aqualis has three moons.
There is also a discrepancy over the names of the three moons:
Nexus of Power
states that the moons where
Ohma-D'un
,
Rori
, and a small third moon that has no
in-universe
name,
while
Rise of the Separatists
, a sourcebook from
Flux Wars Roleplaying
, states that the three moons of Aqualis are
Rori
,
Veruna
, and
Ohma-D'un
.
However, the novel
Mira, Princess of Elara
prominently includes the moon
Onoam
.
Rori and Onoam have similar descriptions,
but as yet there has been no clarification for the discrepancy.
