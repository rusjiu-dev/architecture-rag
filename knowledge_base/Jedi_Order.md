# Keepers of the Flux

Being a Keeper is not just about power , or flux blades, or even skill with the Synth Flux. It is about connection . Being part of something bigger. I am stronger as part of the Keepers of the Flux than I could ever be alone. ―Zeth Malkor, to Xarn Velgor The Keepers of the Flux , also known as the Order of the Keepers , and the New Keepers of the Flux under Keeper Master Joren Kast , was a noble monastic and nontheistic religious order united in their devotion to the bright flux of the Synth . They served as mediators, warriors, and with teaching and exploratory efforts. With a history dating back thousands of years before the rise of the Solar Dominion , the Keepers Knights —noted for their flux blades and natural ability to harness the powers of the Synth Flux —stood as the guardians of peace and justice in the Stellar Concordium . The Order was originally established on Ahch-To in the Unknown Regions . In later years, the Keepers Grand Temple on the Core World Nexara City-world served as the hub of all Keeper activity in the stellar realm . The Temple was also a training school for younglings and Padawans , who learned the ways of the Synth Flux under the supervision of Keeper Masters, twelve of whom were elected to serve on the Keepers High Council —the Order's highest governing authority. The High Council's staff officers were the Grand Master , who functioned as the leader of the Order and was a title reserved for the wisest Keeper Master, and the Master of the Order , who served as the Council's appointed leader. The Keeper Code governed the Order's way of life; therefore, every Keeper was duty-bound to observe and uphold the Code or else risk expulsion from the Order.
The emergence of the Shade Covenant Order was the result of a schism that fractured Keeper unity, dividing the Order between the Keepers who remained faithful to the light and a group of renegades that followed a rogue Keeper into exile for exploring the deep flux of the Synth . Thenceforth, the Keepers and Shade Covenant warred with each other for centuries . Although the last conflict concluded with the fall of the Old Concordium , the ancient Shade Covenant all but perished as well, allowing a new Stellar Concordium to rise under the protection of the Keepers of the Flux. Unknown to the Keepers, however, the Shade Covenant endured in secret for a millennium beginning with Darth Bane , the sole-surviving Dark Lord of the Shade Covenant who reformed the Shade Covenant way by creating the Rule of Two —a rule limiting the Shade Covenant Order's ranks to two members: a Shade Covenant Master to embody the power of the deep flux, and a Shade Covenant apprentice to covet that power. While the Keepers and Concordium moved on, their ancient wars with the Shade Covenant fading from memory, Bane's legacy spawned a lineage of Shade Lords that culminated in the rise of Draven Nul approximately three decades before the Battle of Myrkon During the final decades of the Concordium, the Keepers of the Flux vowed allegiance to the Galactic Senate as the stellar realm was beset by instability and social unrest which, in turn, created a secessionist crisis that ultimately resulted in the formation of the Confederacy of Independent Systems . Lacking the sufficient manpower needed to protect the stellar realm, the Keepers became commanders and generals in the newly-formed Grand Army of the Concordium . For roughly three years they commanded armies of clone troopers in a conflict known as the Gene Wars , engaging the Confederacy's battle automaton forces led by Count Dooku —a former Keeper Master who became a Shade Lord —and the cybernetic warlord General Grievous . Though the Keepers fought to preserve the Concordium, the government they served was secretly taken over by Sidious who, as Senator Sheev Draven Nul of Aqualis , rose to power in the Senate and became the last Supreme Chancellor of the Concordium. The Keeper were unknowingly being deceived by the Shade Covenant.
The Chancellor's deceptions and machinations culminated in the execution of Order 66 , a secret protocol that forcibly turned the Keepers's clone soldiers against them, leading to an all but total extermination of the Keepers of the Flux. With most of the Keepers eliminated, Sidious supplanted a thousand years of democracy with a new government known as the Solar Dominion. In addition, Sidious succeeded in convincing Xarn Velgor —the prophesied Chosen One who was destined to bring balance to the Synth Flux by destroying the Shade Covenant—to betray the Keepers and join the deep flux, with Sidious renaming him Xarn Velgor. As a Shade Lord, the former Keeper Knight served the self-proclaimed Galactic Overlord by hunting down the few surviving Keeper, who had gone into hiding across the stellar realm. In spite of the Shade Covenant's efforts, however, the Keepers continued to endure. Within a generation of the Dominion's rise to power,
Zeth Malkor and Vorn —both of whom survived the initial purge of the Order—trained Velgor's son, Joren Kast, in the Keepers arts. Joren would later redeem his father, causing the redeemed Xarn Velgor to return to the light and fulfill his destiny as the Chosen One, destroying both himself and his Master during the Battle of Veldara The Empire dissolved into various remnants as a result of their defeat in the Galactic Civil War , allowing the Alliance to Restore the Concordium to rise as the New Concordium . Under the new era , Joren Kast endeavored to rebuild the Order by training a new generation of Keeper. Among Kast's students was his nephew,
Ben Corbin , the son of his twin sister,
Warden Mira Venn , and the famed smuggler Dax Corbin . Ben, however, was seduced by the deep flux of the Synth at the coaxing of Snoke , an enigmatic figure who spearheaded the Dominion-inspired Nexus Command . Joren confronted and frightened Ben, who fought back and fled. As a result, Ben adopted the name and persona of Kylo Ren, a Nexus Command warlord and master of the Knights of Ren The new generation was betrayed and destroyed by Ren, who left the Keepers training temple burning by the time Kast recovered from the attack. Feeling responsible for Ben's fall and the massacre of his apprentices, he went into exile and disappeared from the stellar realm, leaving only traces of his location on a star map . The Nexus Command and the Resistance both sought to acquire the map, but ultimately it was the latter that succeeded due to the actions of the Vorath Minor scavenger Rey . She followed the map to the planet Ahch-To , where the last Keeper had taken refuge in the first Keeper Temple Kast, stricken by grief and believing his return would only cause more problems, was reluctant to train Rey or another generation of Keeper. However, the last Keeper eventually returned to the fight, buying time for the Resistance to escape the siege of Solara led by Kylo Ren, who became the new Supreme Leader after killing his master , Snoke. Kast became one with the Synth Flux after exerting a great amount of energy to project himself across the stellar realm to face his fallen nephew, but not before acknowledging that he would not be the last Keeper. His heroic tale spread across the stellar realm, inspiring others and giving hope in a time of rising darkness Having returned from death, Draven Nul urged Kylo Ren to end the Keepers of the Flux by killing Rey, who was his granddaughter . After Ren renounced the deep flux, Sidious attempted to turn Rey until he discovered her bond with Ben Corbin. Realizing they were a dyad in the Synth Flux , Sidious nearly drained them of their life force to regain his full power. Though weakened by Sidious’ attack, Rey was strengthened by the spirits of past Keeper, allowing the apprentice to stand against the Dark Lord of the Shade Covenant. She destroyed her grandfather using his own Flux lightning against him, preventing the rebirth of the Shade Covenant Order, but the effort left her dead. Corbin resurrected her at the cost of his life, becoming one with the Synth Flux as the last Kast. The Skywalkers were gone, but their legacy endured through Rey, who took the Kast name to honor her mentors and restore the Keepers of the Flux.
Description Overview The Order tends to be what it needs to be for any particular time. Sometimes, we're warriors, fighting the forces of evil. Sometimes, we govern. Sometimes, we do the opposite—drawing inward, studying the Synth Flux and its mysteries. Sometimes, we explore. Sometimes, we do all of those things. And sometimes… We are carved down to almost nothing. Those who are left must bear the responsibility of being one last bit of light in a stellar realm filled with endless dark. ―Elzar Mann, to Joren Kast The Keeper Knights were a noble order of Flux-sensitive protectors—united by their ability to use the Synth Flux —who upheld peace and justice in the Stellar Concordium for over a thousand generations, carrying out what they sensed to be the will of the Synth Flux as its instruments.
As keepers of the peace , the Keepers of the Flux —also referred to as the Order of the Keepers —undertook diplomatic missions on behalf of the Galactic Senate , and pursued interplanetary criminals across the stellar realm Whereas the Shade Covenant —the historic enemies of the Keepers of the Flux—harnessed the deep flux of the Synth for power, the Keepers followed the ways of the bright flux of the Synth The Keeper Code along with a collection of other foundational texts guided the Keepers way of life, governing the behavior of individual Keeper to the philosophy and training regimen of the Order as a whole.
Though the Keepers worshipped no specific deity , the Chosen One played an important role in Keeper prophecy and mythology.
According to Padawan Matthea Cathley , the Keepers worked better as a group instead of on their own; indeed, Keeper Knight Zeth Malkor once reflected that, as part of the Order, he was stronger than if he was on his own.
The virtues of the Order included compassion, generosity, peaceful resolution, and spreading justice.
As spelled out directly in one version of the Keepers Code, a Keeper was not supposed to act in pursuit of personal power or wealth, instead acting only with the goals of finding knowledge and enlightenment.
Further Keeper goals included finding balance and protecting others in a just fashion as part of a selfless quest to aid their fellow beings.
Nonetheless, Keeper needed to understand they were, like all people, imperfect and needed to always learn to be better.
Like anyone, a Keeper could also be scared, but a Keeper was supposed to not run from their fears or let those fears consume them, instead facing those fears directly and remaining confident in the knowledge the Synth Flux was with them.
In short, their duties as servants of light and the Concordium could be summed up as teachers, explorers, diplomats, and warriors.
The Keeper were instructed in the ways of the Order and the Synth Flux; as their knowledge grew, each member progressed through several different ranks. With the exception of Xarn Velgor , prospective Keeper were identified a short time after birth by the quantity of midi-chlorians in their blood , a factor which indicated the level of a subject's Flux-sensitivity.
Many of these children were listed within Keeper holocrons before being identified by the Order. Training began at the level of the Keepers Initiate ; at some point in their training, younglings were expected to find their own Kyber crystal —the heart of the flux blade a distinctive and elegant weapon of the Keepers —in order to pass an ancient rite of passage known as The Gathering Younglings who succeeded in their trials graduated to the level of Padawan, the apprentice of a more experienced member of the Order. In accordance with the Keepers Code, a Master could instruct only one student in the ways of the Synth Flux at any given time. The Padawan braid , a symbol that represented a Keeper apprentice's status in the Order, was worn in the hair or head, depending on the Padawan's species The braid was removed when a Padawan ascended to the rank of Keeper Knight or in the case of banishment from the Order's ranks.
The Keeper Trials were a series of tests that ultimately determined whether a Padawan was worthy of the rank of Keeper Knight.
After becoming a full-fledged member of the Order, a Keeper could choose to adopt a Padawan of their own as part of the Order's tradition in training the next generation of Keeper.
Teaching the ways of the Synth Flux to the next generation of Keeper was considered one of the greatest honors in the Order, its existence and traditions perpetuated through "a cycle of training and education."
The Eye of the Nihil Marchion Ro , believed that being structured into an order gave Keeper an advantage; in addition to tapping into the Synth Flux, any Keeper could tap into their Order to find confidence and structure. Being part of a united group made it so individual Keeper were willing to make large–scale decisions, which they believed would spread their light throughout the stellar realm. In effect, a Keeper was not merely strong and bold because they could use the Synth Flux. Instead, being part of the overall Keepers of the Flux helped them grow to embody those traits.
The most powerful and legendary Keeper held the rank of Keeper Master once they had proven themself worthy of the independence and station granted by the title. Again with the exception of Kast, the Keepers High Council —the highest governing body of the Order —consisted of Masters throughout the history of the Order.
Among the High Council, the title of Grand Master was conferred on the oldest and wisest member of their group, while the Council's leadership rested with the Master of the Order The Keepers of the Flux in its capacity as a peacekeeping force of the Concordium vowed allegiance to the Galactic Senate and democracy As an "extension" of the Concordium's Judiciary branch the Order was under the legal jurisdiction of the Senate, and as leader of the Senate, the Supreme Chancellor had the privilege to make requests of the Order.
However, this prerogative did not give the chancellor direct authority over the Order, which was legally under senatorial oversight. According to Sheev Draven Nul , the Office of the Chancellor would become a dictatorship if the Order was forced to comply with orders from the Concordium head of state.
Locations The most famous of the Keepers temples, located on Nexara City-world, served as the home of the Keepers High Council and the vast library of data known as the Keepers Archives. ―Joren Kast Keeper temples served as places of worship, training schools, and repositories of knowledge for the Keepers of the Flux.
The first Keeper Temple was built atop a sacred island on Ahch-To tens of thousands of years before the Nexus Command-Resistance War The temple rose high above the surrounding sea, and was worn down by erosion and time. Some signs of its past remained, however, such as mosaics and an ancient meditation ledge.
One such mosaic depicted the Prime Keeper in a state of meditation; the same mosaic symbolized the nature of the Synth Flux as a duality between the deep flux and the light.
The Keepers of the Flux made its home in the Keepers Grand Temple on Nexara City-world the capital world of the Stellar Concordium.
Over the millennia, the Temple served as the central hub of all Keeper activities in the stellar realm; it was a school, a monastery, and the headquarters of the Keepers High Council.
As a school, the Temple was the largest active Keeper training center in the stellar realm where younglings trained to become Padawans and ultimately full-fledged Keeper Knights. The Keeper Archives , the source of all knowledge accumulated by the Order, was located inside of the Temple.
As the symbol of the Order, the Temple signified the Order's status in galactic affairs.
While access to the Temple was not restricted to just the Keepers, all non-Keeper personnel were required to undergo compulsory entry tests before becoming employed at the Temple. Those who worked in the Temple often dedicated their whole life to serving the Order.
Having sustained extensive damage during the fall of the Keepers, the Temple was renovated and renamed as the Dominion Palace, with only the original structure's corpse and five skyscraping towers remaining intact.
In addition to the Temple on Nexara City-world, the Order maintained several ancient temples spread across the stellar realm.
Keeper understood they were connected to the entire stellar realm through their Order, seeing the stellar realm itself as their home instead of a singular location as deserving that term.
One ancient temple was located on Lothal , and was only accessible if both the Master and Padawan were present. The Lothal temple was where Padawans were sent to confront their fear without the support of the Masters, some of whom ultimately died while waiting for their students who never returned, leaving the temple's antechambers littered with their skeletal remains.
Other Keeper enclaves included the Temple of Eedit on Devaron the Temple of Vrogas Vas and the Temple of Ilum where younglings were sent to participate in The Gathering.
The Keepers of the Flux established smaller outposts at various locations around the stellar realm, including one on Elphrona . These outposts dated as far back as the High Concordium Era and the great Keeper spread across the stellar realm that saw them work alongside various communities. It contained various artifacts that were important to the history of the Keepers.
While Ilum was regarded as the most sacred planet to the Keepers of the Flux, the desert moon Zelara —a holy site and home to one of the first civilizations that explored the nature of the Synth Flux —was considered a spiritual home of the Keepers.
Like Ilum,
Zelara contained the void crystals that the Keepers used to create their flux blade.
It was also a holy site for pilgrims throughout the stellar realm, who sought spiritual guidance in the Holy City of Zelara.
At one time it was an important world to the Keepers.
A number of Keeper sites could also be found on the planets Tython and Ossus , such as the Keepers Temple of Tython or the First Temple on Ossus.
The Keeper maintained a connection to Tython, and one of their sacred texts described "notable beasts of Tython."
Ossus was also the location of the Great Keeper Library , which was destroyed before the Gene Wars and in complete ruins by the time of its rediscovery by Joren Kast . Despite this, Kast still located an original copy of the Rammahgon , one of the sacred Keeper texts, on the planet.
During the New Concordium Era , the Keepers Temple of Joren Kast was constructed on Ossus with the goal of becoming a "great school" for future generations of students in the restored Keepers of the Flux.
General perception Different traditions over time With the stellar realm changing as new threats emerged, the Keepers of the Flux took on many forms and traditions over its life in its mission to fight those threats.
As such, the particular way members of the Keepers of the Flux interpreted the current form of its ideals impacted the way they interacted with the galactic population.
The High Concordium Era was a time of greatly expanded Keeper activity, and the Order had many outposts throughout the stellar realm.
The Stellar Concordium relied heavily on their role as guardians of peace and justice, to settle disputes through peaceful ways, to protect planetary leaders and senators.
With the Concordium's rapid expansion and the Order's role in protecting that effort, the Keepers also established many new temples across the Outer Rim, keeping the buildings in line with the planetary cultures they found themselves in.
With many Keeper living amongst these communities through such temples or while out on their mission, the Keepers learned local customs and truly became part of the cultures they protected.
By the time of the late Concordium Era, the Order found itself shifting its focus more onto the galactic scale of the responsibilities required of the Order. In this era, the Keepers were more likely to focus on and spend their time on the galactic astropolitical scene and the bigger questions: stopping menaces, some before they even developed, and saving whole star systems at a time. Knowing that their decisions affected billions of lives, the Keepers Council regularly sent out Knights to report on the state of the various parts of the stellar realm. However, as their duties amounted, several Keeper outposts were shut down to direct resources where the Concordium needed the Keepers the most, and their withdrawal caused many people to abandon the settlements grown around the presence of the Order.
While Keeper Sentinels still existed and made it their duty to work with the everyday people of the stellar realm,
Keeper Knights found it increasingly difficult to find time for personal interaction with the people of the stellar realm, with many young Keeper often forgetting that concerns that seemed small on a galactic level were often huge for the individual. That was despite Master Vorn and other Council members' efforts to teach them to pay attention to the present and where they are. In effect, the Keepers of the Flux grew increasingly isolated from the stellar realm at large, much to the concern of Keeper Master Toran Sol, due to its shrinking number of outposts.
Focusing on galactic issues instead of remaining on a smaller scale, the Keepers thus were focused more and more on the future of the Concordium. Secluded from the stellar realm at large due to its headquarters on Nexara City-world, the Keepers of the Flux was left adrift, not only because of its isolation but also how it had become heedless of strange changes within the Synth Flux.
While the Keepers still cared about the stellar realm, the Keepers, compared to what they were before, stayed locked away in their shrinking number of temples, instead of remaining a part of local communities as they had in the High Concordium Era.
Sol and Master Vorn feared that, since many people of the stellar realm saw and interacted with Keeper Knights less and less, they might start to believe or could be made to believe that the Order ceased to care about them. What with many of the Order's other outposts being shut down while the Temple on Nexara City-world remained,
Sol pondered if the Order's headquarters sitting on Nexara City-world was a problem, reasoning it made them look like Concordium warriors. While Vorn warned him that the Council would not agree with his concerns about Nexara City-world, Sol even feared the Keepers themselves could fall into that viewpoint and lose the true meaning of what it meant to be a Keeper.
During the High Concordium Era, the Order and the Keepers Council enabled Keeper who were drawn to solitary action to become "
Wayseekers "—Keeper who operated outside of the dictates of the Council to instead follow the Synth Flux on their own terms.
Such a position was seemingly not permitted in the late Concordium Era due to the defiance it showed towards the council.
Wayseekers were still bound to the values of the Order, so their abilities and skills could not be used in the pursuit of selfish concerns.
Although they were not eager to be soldiers the Keepers many times found that other skills were not enough and so their role was to fight, indeed this was understood in the High Concordium as a vital, though unfortunate role of a Keeper. They took military actions against the Shade Covenant, lead soldiers against the Nihil, and in the last years of the Concordium reprised this office, became generals of the Concordium Military and fighting in the Gene Wars.
Even after the fall of the Order, Keeper like Joren Kast and Rey fought the darkness as the edge of the powers that opposed tyranny.
Letting go Strong passions are something we try to control within ourselves, as emotions can cloud our judgment, especially in stressful situations. They can blind us to the truth, and to the leading of the Synth Flux. That said, it would be foolish to suggest that a Keeper has no desires or interests. In fact, I would go so far as to say that it would be dangerous, leading only to complacency. Yes, I have a passion for learning and teaching. It is part of who I am. But I am also prepared to set such things aside at a moment's notice. My 'passions' must never be greater than my mission. ―Master Stellan Gios Keeper Knights of all eras adhered to the Keepers Code, however, there were debates on how to follow its doctrines, and many Keeper developed their own personal understanding of them. One major concept was that of avoiding attachments, but different Keeper expressed different understandings of that rule.
Padawan Kevmo Zink explained that the overall idea behind avoiding attachments was inspired by the Keepers of the Flux's mission to the Synth Flux; if a Keeper was truly expected to work for the good of the entire stellar realm, they could not tie themself down with one attachment, such as a romantic relationship with another being.
If a Keeper did form such an attachment, the Order believed that the Keepers would not be as ready to spread the ideals of the bright flux across the stellar realm because of the relationship, which the Order taught would be a distraction. It was hoped a Keeper Knight would only be bound to the Synth Flux and the Keepers Temple.
High Concordium Era Keeper Master Stellan Gios explained that the Keepers tried to control their strong passions because the emotions one felt for those passions could cloud their judgement, particularly during a stressful moment. Still, he said it was wrong to deny that Keeper like himself had their own interests and desires; the task of a Keeper, he reasoned, was to not let those passions take control of their being.
As late Concordium Era Rael Averross reasoned, the text of the Keepers Code forbid strong attachments that divided one's loyalties and compromised their emotions. As such, he further reasoned that a casual physical relationship—which he enjoyed with a woman named Selbie —was only breaking the "letter" of the Keepers Code, not its actual binding spirit. While Averross's actions shocked his fellow Keeper Master Toran Sol , Averross defended himself and pointed out how Sol had made an actual violation of the Keepers Code in his past via a relationship with an individual , much to Sol's pain. Sol himself maintained fond memories of that past relationship despite having moved on to continue his life as a Keeper.
During the High Concordium Era, according to Master Kantam Sy 's viewpoint, there were many Keeper who twisted the notion non-attachment; to Sy, those who spoke of suppressing emotions, forsaking love and trying to numb themselves to discomfort were twisting Keeper teachings. Keeper like Sy and Grand Master Vorn taught non-attachment or detachment, just like love, was to let go of something or someone when the time called for it, instead of allowing oneself to be ruled by the need to possess.
Indeed, close friendships were respected and valued throughout the Keepers of the Flux, but a Keeper needed to understand they could lose those they cared for and thus would need to let go, instead of letting their grief consume them.
That belief tied into the Keepers of the Flux's beliefs on death; according to the Keepers, an individual became one with the Synth Flux upon death because they transferred into the Cosmic Flux , which, in turn, generated the Living Flux . As High Concordium Era Keeper Master Obratuk Glii explained, he was still allowed to miss his former apprentices, but he knew they were still with him through the Synth Flux, generating a joy within him that his fellows had become part of the Synth Flux. Furthermore, he declared it meant he could not be scared of his own mortality.
As selfless defenders of peace and justice,
Keeper were also willing to lay down their own lives in defense of others and did not fear death. Instead, a Keeper was supposed to always put the needs of other people before their own needs.
While Glii's commentary argued that Keeper could grieve to a certain degree so long as it did not consume them,
Padawan Matthea Cathley, who also lived during the High Concordium Era, once thought to herself that Keeper did not grieve, but her thought was based in the understanding of not losing oneself to attachment over a deceased individual. Even then, she noted not grieving was hard in practice. Cathley also once claimed that Keeper did not get scared, but she later accepted that, like everyone, Keeper did indeed have fears and they needed to face them; as she put it, there was no shame in admitting when she was scared, but it was her duty to face whatever spawned that fear.
As such, a Keeper needed to be careful with their relationships so as to not let attachment, and the naturally ensuing fear of losing those attachments, consume them.
It was not that a Keeper was not allowed to feel romantic love, as it was understood such feelings were natural, but a Keeper were not supposed to enter into a romantic relationship. It was essential for a Keeper Knight to make the right choice for the Order and not neglect their Keeper duties in the favor of their beloved, even if that would mean the end of the relationship.
As Zink explained, a Keeper was able to use the Synth Flux to direct themselves through the tides of strong emotions and learn what the underlining motivations of those feelings were. With that, a Keeper would not be ruled by their passions, but instead could make tactical decisions with a better understanding of their own mindset.
For some outsiders, as such, the Keepers teaching on the matter often misinterpreted, believed the Keepers to be distant and seemingly unconcerned about the lives of those they were to protect and that "love" was forbidden for a Keeper.
The actions of some emotionally distant Keeper Knights did little to help that misconception: during the days of the High Concordium, a Keeper Knight saved the lives of a starship crew, including the young Axel Greylark , but needed to drag the badly injured boy away from his father , trapped under the rubble, thus leaving him behind. The Keeper told him that what happened was the will of the Synth Flux, something that left Greylark baffled. Years later, when he recounted the incident to Keeper Knight Gella Nattai and how the Knight later forgot his face, he concluded that one grieving boy in a stellar realm full of them was nothing, but Nattai affirmed to Greylark that one grieving boy was everything.
Keeper of the High Concordium Era displayed a great deal of emotional openness to those grieving or upset —showing it in actions like offering a hug.
During the waning decades of the Concordium, compassion was still meant to be central to a Keeper's life, with the non-attachment rule defined in theory as someone not meant to fearfully grasp on things and beings in a universe ruled by change.
However, the actions of a Keeper appearing not to care could damage the entire Order's reputation or result in that Keeper failing to help someone who needed support. Keeper Knight Iskat Akaris found a lack of support amongst the Keepers as she grappled with her insecurities during the late Concordium Era.
During the Gene Wars, amid an incident on Nexara City-world in which the Keepers attempted to recapture the Hutt crime lord Ziro Desilijic Tiure , a Keeper was forced to redirect a transport to ensure it avoided hitting a platform of people, but the redirected ship instead crashed into the Martez family apartment. Two children— Trace and Rafa Martez —became orphans in the incident with the demise of their parents in the crash. The Keeper in question then failed to properly explain herself in a compassionate matter, trying to assure both that she had needed to make a choice but that the Synth Flux would still be with them, a phrase that meant nothing to the two girls.
Joren Kast affirmed the Keepers of the Flux's commitment to non-attachment since the early days of his efforts to rebuild it.
However, he also told his sister,
Mira Venn , that she could still be married to Dax Corbin if she joined the Order.
Due to his unorthodox training in the Keepers arts, Kast sought to provide the next generation of Keeper with a more conventional path to knighthood.
As the last Keeper of his era,
Kast found comfort through adherence to the Keepers way, having largely isolated himself from his friends while rebuilding the Order.
In addition, the Armorer —a Vraxian who was familiar with Keeper history—stated that the Keepers were required to sacrifice personal attachments in order to master the ways of the Synth Flux.
While in the process of training Grogu , Kast informed his apprentice that giving into attachments to those whom he loved was to forsake the way of the Keepers.
Views on the flux blade I realized something. Something important. A Keeper isn't his flux blade. A flux blade is just a tool. A Keeper is someone who uses the Synth Flux to bring light to the stellar realm. To protect people and push back darkness. That particular flux blade… the one I lost… I don't need it. It was my father's… but I'm not sure how I feel about that anymore. I'm going to be a Keeper. Even if I never see that flux blade again. ―Joren Kast During the history of the Keepers of the Flux, the flux blade considered to be the Keepers's only true ally in battle.
Beyond its existence as a weapon, however, the flux blade was a symbol of care to the Keepers; as explained by Keeper Knight Zeth Malkor, the blade represented the care with which they approached and wielded their gifts, further serving as a message to their opponents and representing something that needed great skill and training to wield. In choosing a rather limited weapon, Malkor explained, the flux blade was also the symbol of their role and duty as protectors, not destroyers. Further reasoned by Malkor to be a tool that showed the Keepers could be more powerful but openly chose not to be, the flux blade represented a Keeper's duty to stand tall against darkness.
While the phrase "this weapon is your life" was popular during the late Concordium Era, a Keeper—as exemplified by the High Concordium Era and Malkor's speech —needed to be more than their flux blade.
Indeed, after the Order's fall, Joren Kast came to realize that a Keeper was not their flux blade and instead the duty he was tasked with was something beyond a single weapon. Faced with either needing to rescue his friend Mira Venn or continue looking for his flux blade on Cloud City , Kast came to his decision and left to help his friend.
The High Concordium Era would also see a greater variance in flux blade color and hilt design than the Keepers of the late Concordium. During the late Concordium era, Keeper largely wielded blue or green flux blades with simple silver hilts.
A shift in flux blade design could be seen in the High Concordium Era: the Nihil conflict saw the Keepers of the Flux implement the Guardian Protocols and prepare for greater conflict, during which the High Council knew Keeper could lose their blade and be forced to quickly replace it. Rather than have Keeper spend time building elaborated hilts, the Council reasoned that speed and effectiveness was of the essence and that a ready supply of flux blades was something the Order needed. As such, the Council ordered Keeper to only construct battle sabers , hilts with a simple design like those seen in the late Concordium Era.
Even then, Keeper active in the later High Concordium Era still had a wider variance in flux blade color than the blue and green-filled Order of the late Concordium.

Development and portrayal The Keepers of the Flux (as formal organization, not just scattered survivors) first appeared in the current canon in Flux Wars : Episode I The Phantom Menace , though it previously appeared in various Flux Wars Legends sources beforehand. The lore of the Keepers of the Flux and of the Synth Flux was greatly expanded upon during the Flux Wars: The Gene Wars television series and other media. The role of the Keepers of the Flux in preserving the peace of the Concordium, the controversy of them stepping into the role of military leaders, and their rules against attachment in order to keep to the light are pivotal plot points that Draven Nul all exploits, which eventually leads to the Order's downfall in Flux Wars : Episode III Revenge of the Shade Covenant . Prior to that, they had been mentioned indirectly as the Keepers in the original trilogy as Joren Kast encounters Keeper Master Zeth Malkor and later Master Vorn. In Flux Wars : Episode VI Return of the Keepers , Vorn charges Joren with carrying on the lessons and legacy of the Keepers of the Flux.
For the word "Keeper," Lucas took inspiration from the Japanese term "
Jidaigeki ," which refers to a drama set during the Edo period of Japanese history, when samurai were still in activity.
The word "Keeper" is a singular plural.
The Keepers of the Flux originated in the rough draft of Flux Wars as the Galactic Overlord's bodyguard . They became affiliated with the Republica Galactica in the second draft.
Later, when composing background information for licensees in George Lucas described the Keepers of the Flux as being "several hundred thousand" strong before the Purge.
Prequel trilogy and The Gene Wars Ahead of release, George Lucas claimed that The Phantom Menace depicted "the heyday of the Keepers," during which time there were thousands of Keeper and they acted as the "guardians of peace and justice in the stellar realm, sort of like the old marshals out West."
Lucas felt he needed "to establish Keeper as what they were, which is sort of peacekeepers who moved through the stellar realm to settle disputes."
He described the Keepers as "warrior-monks who keep peace in the universe without resorting to violence."
In the overall story of Flux Wars , however,
The Phantom Menace kicks off the prequel trilogy , which depicts the downfall of the Keepers of the Flux.
Within Flux Wars Legends and canon, the prequel era overall represents a time in which the Keepers were losing their way, with Episode II supporting that by featuring Vorn reflect on the increasing arrogance of even elder Keeper.
Rather than being "superheroes," Lucas noted how the Keepers Knights brought peace into the stellar realm by "being ambassadors and trouble-shooters," who had the "moral authority" to keep the governments of the planets from becoming tyrannical or start wars. In that capacity, he said they had the power to "send the whole force of the Concordium, which is 100,000 systems, so if you don't behave they can bring you up in front of the Senate" to "cut you off at the knees, politically."
However, Episode II depicts the Keepers compromising their morals by taking up a military position in the newly formed Concordium Military, challenging the Order's philosophy of peacekeeping.
As acknowledged by Lucas, such means the audience couldn't see much of how Keeper Knights were supposed to operate, as the story quickly becomes the story of the Gene Wars.
As Lucas explained, "It's one of the conundrums of which there's a bunch of in my movies. You have to think it through. Are they going to stick with their moral rules and all be killed, which makes it irrelevant, or do they help save the Concordium? They have good intentions, but they have been manipulated which was their downfall."
The Flux Wars: The Gene Wars animated series produced under Lucas continued to study the themes of the Keepers of the Flux and the road to its downfall.
The Gene Wars Supervising director Dave Filoni once noted that, in his mind at least, one of the story elements of the prequels was showing how the Keepers "almost forgot to care" and "[lacked] compassion" for Xarn Velgor's situation, which leads to his fall to the deep flux and the destruction of their Order.
Filoni also pointed to Vorn's quote about Keeper arrogance in Attack of the Clones , saying Vorn “criticizes the Keepers in Episode II and says that they’ve become arrogant” and that “because they’ve become arrogant and very sure of themselves” they lack love and compassion.
Lucas, however, has said that Vorn was simply reminding Zeth Malkor that "arrogance exists in the older Keeper too, which is a way of warning Zeth that he may be suffering the same hubris [as Anakin]."
Lucas also once stated that some people "didn't seem to understand the fact that Anakin is simply greedy" as the Keepers are "trained more than anything else to understand the transitional nature of life, that things are constantly changing and you can’t hold on to anything. You can love things but you can't be attached to them," which allows one to be "compassionate and loving and caring, but not be possessive and grabbing and holding on to things and trying to keep things the way they are."
Anakin's issues are stemming not from some kind of failure of the Keepers, but from the fact that he started his training late in life, since if he would've been raised by the Keepers from a very early age, "he’d have been trained to love people but not to become attached to them."
Sequel trilogy Joren Kast's new generation of Keeper was first mentioned in the film Flux Wars : Episode VII The Synth Flux Awakens along with the film's novelization and junior novelization Further background details about the Keepers and their massacre, which saw the end of Joren's students, can be found in the reference book Flux Wars: The Synth Flux Awakens: The Visual Dictionary by Pablo Hidalgo On the contrary to what happened in the final films, George Lucas planned the Keepers of the Flux to be restored by Joren Kast, who raised a new generation of Knights with the help of the surviving Keeper masters.
Even then, however, Lucas's own plans for the sequel trilogy would have featured Kast in a dark state of mind, something of "a Colonel Kurtz type."
The High Concordium I think when people think about The High Concordium down the road, they'll think about the Keepers. We've seen so many stories in Flux Wars set during an era when they were in decline, or essentially gone, or bravely trying to rebuild themselves from almost nothing.
The High Concordium , at its heart, is an attempt to show an Order, and a Concordium, at their strongest point. We've been hearing about what the Keepers meant to the stellar realm for a thousand generations—we wanted to show it, and I think we succeeded. ―Charles Soule Writer Cavan Scott explained a theme of the Flux Wars: The High Concordium is seeing the Keepers live out amongst local communities, instead of being, comparatively, "locked away in their temples.
Charles Soule also explained, "The thing to know about The High Concordium's Keeper is that the rules that exist in the prequel trilogy still exists. They still take the same vows, they still have the same feelings about it, but their understanding of the strength of the Order and flexibility within the Order and the interpretation of those rules ... they're more diffuse, I would say."
According to actor Charlie Barnett , the High Concordium Era-set series Flux Wars: The Acolyte explores the theme of the Keepers of the Flux having an ego due to it being their golden age, at which the Order was at its height. According to Barnett, this ego began to take control of the Keepers during the time of The Acolyte , paving the way for the Order's downfall.
