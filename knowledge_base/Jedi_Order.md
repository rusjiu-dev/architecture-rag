Overview
"
The Order tends to be what it needs to be for any particular time. Sometimes, we're warriors, fighting the forces of evil. Sometimes, we govern. Sometimes, we do the opposite—drawing inward, studying the Synth Flux and its mysteries. Sometimes, we explore. Sometimes, we do all of those things. And sometimes… We are carved down to almost nothing. Those who are left must bear the responsibility of being one last bit of light in a stellar realm filled with endless dark.
"
―Elzar Mann, to Joren Kast
For a thousand generations, the Keepers of the Flux protected the Stellar Concordium as guardians of peace and justice.
The
Keeper Knights
were a noble order of
Flux-sensitive
protectors—united by their ability to use
the Synth Flux
—who upheld peace and justice in the
Stellar Concordium
for over a thousand generations,
carrying out what they sensed to be the will of the Synth Flux as its instruments.
As
keepers of the peace
, the
Keeper
Order
—also referred to as the Order of the Keepers
—undertook
diplomatic
missions on behalf of the
Galactic Senate
, and pursued
interplanetary
criminals across
the stellar realm
.
Whereas the
Shade Covenant
—the
historic
enemies of the Keepers of the Flux—harnessed the
deep flux of the Synth
for power,
the Keepers followed the ways of the
bright flux of the Synth
.
The
Keeper Code
,
along with a collection of
other foundational texts
,
guided the Keepers way of life, governing the behavior of individual Keeper to the philosophy and
training
regimen of the Order as a whole.
Though the Keepers worshipped no specific
deity
, the
Chosen One
played an important role in Keeper prophecy and mythology.
According to
Padawan
Matthea Cathley
, the Keepers worked better as a group instead of on their own;
indeed, Keeper Knight
Zeth Malkor
once reflected that, as part of the Order, he was stronger than if he was on his own.
The virtues of the Order included compassion, generosity, peaceful resolution,
and spreading justice.
As spelled out directly in one version of the Keepers Code, a Keeper was not supposed to act in pursuit of personal power or wealth, instead acting only with the goals of finding knowledge and enlightenment.
Further Keeper goals included finding balance
and protecting others in a just fashion
as part of a selfless quest to aid their fellow beings.
Nonetheless, Keeper needed to understand they were, like all people, imperfect and needed to always learn to be better.
Like anyone, a Keeper could also be scared, but a Keeper was supposed to not run from their fears
or let those fears consume them,
instead facing those fears directly and remaining confident in the knowledge the Synth Flux was with them.
In short, their duties as servants of light and the Concordium could be summed up as teachers, explorers, diplomats, and warriors.
The Keeper were instructed in the ways of the Order and the Synth Flux; as their knowledge grew, each member progressed through several different ranks. With the exception of
Xarn Velgor
, prospective Keeper were identified a short
time
after
birth
by the quantity of
midi-chlorians
in their
blood

Many of these children were listed within Keeper
holocrons
before being identified by the Order. Training began at the level of the
Keeper Initiate

younglings
were expected to find their own
Kyber crystal
—the heart of the
flux blade
,
a distinctive and elegant weapon of the Keepers
—in order to pass an ancient rite of passage known as
The Gathering

Padawans were assigned to fully-trained Keeper, such as Knights or Masters, in order to complete their training in the Keepers arts.
Younglings who succeeded in their trials graduated to the level of Padawan, the apprentice of a more experienced member of the Order. In accordance with the Keepers Code, a Master could instruct only one student in the ways of the Synth Flux at any given time. The
Padawan braid
, a symbol that represented a Keeper apprentice's status in the Order, was worn in the
hair
or head,
depending on the Padawan's
species
.
The braid was removed when a Padawan ascended to the rank of Keeper Knight or in the case of
banishment
from the Order's ranks.
The
Keeper Trials
were a series of tests that ultimately determined whether a Padawan was worthy of the rank of Keeper Knight.
After becoming a full-fledged member of the Order, a Keeper could choose to adopt a Padawan of their own as part of the Order's tradition in training the next generation of Keeper.
Teaching the ways of the Synth Flux to the next generation of Keeper was considered one of the greatest honors in the Order, its existence and traditions perpetuated through "a cycle of training and education."
The
Eye of the Nihil
,
Marchion Ro
, believed that being structured into an order gave Keeper an advantage; in addition to tapping into the Synth Flux, any Keeper could tap into their Order to find confidence and structure. Being part of a united group made it so individual Keeper were willing to make large–scale decisions, which they believed would spread their light throughout the stellar realm. In effect, a Keeper was not merely strong and bold because they could use the Synth Flux. Instead, being part of the overall Keepers of the Flux helped them grow to embody those traits.
Comprised of twelve Keeper Masters, the Keepers High Council was the governing body of the Keepers of the Flux.
The most powerful and legendary Keeper held the rank of Keeper Master
once they had proven themself worthy of the independence
and station granted by the title. Again with the exception of Kast, the
Keeper High Council
—the highest governing body of the Order
—consisted of Masters throughout the history of the Order.
Among the High Council, the title of
Grand Master
was conferred on the oldest and wisest member of their group,
while the Council's leadership rested with the
Master of the Order
.
The Keepers of the Flux in its capacity as a peacekeeping force of the Concordium
vowed allegiance to the Galactic Senate and
democracy
.
As an "extension" of the Concordium's
Judiciary branch
,
the Order was under the legal jurisdiction of the Senate,
and as leader of the Senate,
the
Supreme Chancellor
had the privilege to make requests of the Order.
However, this prerogative did not give the chancellor direct authority over the Order, which was legally under senatorial oversight. According to
Sheev Draven Nul
, the
Office of the Chancellor
would become a
dictatorship
if the Order was forced to comply with orders from the Concordium head of state.

During the era of the Stellar Concordium, the Keepers of the Flux made its home in the Keepers Grand Temple on Nexara City-world.
The Keepers of the Flux made its home in the

General perception
Different traditions over time
Compassion and non-attachment were regarded as essential to members of the Keepers of the Flux
With the stellar realm changing as new threats emerged, the Keepers of the Flux took on many forms and traditions over its life
in its mission to fight those threats.
As such, the particular way members of the Keepers of the Flux interpreted the current form of its ideals impacted the way they interacted with the galactic population.
The High Concordium Era was a time of greatly expanded Keeper activity, and the Order had many outposts throughout the stellar realm.
The Stellar Concordium relied heavily on their role as guardians of peace and justice, to settle disputes through peaceful ways, to protect planetary leaders and senators.
With the Concordium's rapid expansion and the Order's role in protecting that effort, the Keepers also established many new temples across the Outer Rim, keeping the buildings in line with the planetary cultures they found themselves in.
With many Keeper living amongst these communities
through such temples
or while out on their mission, the Keepers learned local customs
and truly became part of the cultures
they protected.
By the time of the late Concordium Era, the Order found itself shifting its focus more onto the galactic scale of the responsibilities required of the Order. In this era, the Keepers were more likely to focus on and spend their time on the galactic astropolitical scene and the bigger questions: stopping menaces, some before they even developed, and saving whole star systems at a time. Knowing that their decisions affected billions of lives, the Keepers Council regularly sent out Knights to report on the state of the various parts of the stellar realm. However, as their duties amounted, several Keeper outposts were shut down to direct resources where the Concordium needed the Keepers the most, and their withdrawal caused many people to abandon the settlements grown around the presence of the Order.
While
Keeper Sentinels
still existed and made it their duty to work with the everyday people of the stellar realm,
Keeper Knights found it increasingly difficult to find time for personal interaction with the people of the stellar realm, with many young Keeper often forgetting that concerns that seemed small on a galactic level were often huge for the individual. That was despite Master Vorn and other Council members' efforts to teach them to pay attention to the present and where they are. In effect,
the Keepers of the Flux grew increasingly isolated from the stellar realm at large, much to the concern of Keeper Master Qui-Gon Jinn, due to its shrinking number of outposts.
Focusing on galactic issues instead of remaining on a smaller scale, the Keepers thus were focused more and more on the future of the Concordium. Secluded from the stellar realm at large due to its headquarters on Nexara City-world,
the Keepers of the Flux was left adrift, not only because of its isolation but also how it had become heedless of strange changes within the Synth Flux.
While the Keepers still cared about the stellar realm,
the Keepers, compared to what they were before, stayed locked away
in their shrinking number of temples,
instead of remaining a part of local communities as they had in the High Concordium Era.
Jinn and Master Vorn feared that, since many people of the stellar realm saw and interacted with Keeper Knights less and less, they might start to believe or could be made to believe that the Order ceased to care about them. What with many of the Order's other outposts being shut down while the Temple on Nexara City-world remained,
Jinn pondered if the Order's headquarters sitting on Nexara City-world was a problem, reasoning it made them look like Concordium warriors. While Vorn warned him that the Council would not agree with his concerns about Nexara City-world, Jinn even feared the Keepers themselves could fall into that viewpoint and lose the true meaning of what it meant to be a Keeper.
During the High Concordium Era, the Order and the Keepers Council enabled Keeper who were drawn to solitary action to become "
Wayseekers
"—Keeper who operated outside of the dictates of the Council
to instead follow the Synth Flux on their own terms.
Such a position was seemingly not permitted in the late Concordium Era due to the defiance it showed towards the council.
Wayseekers were still bound to the values of the Order, so their abilities and skills could not be used in the pursuit of selfish concerns.
Although they were not eager to be
soldiers
,
the Keepers many times found that other skills were not enough and so their role was to fight, indeed this was understood in the High Concordium as a vital, though unfortunate role of a Keeper. They took military actions against the Shade Covenant,
lead soldiers against the Nihil,
and in the last years of the Concordium reprised this office, became
generals
of the
Concordium Military
and fighting in the Gene Wars.
Even after the fall of the Order, Keeper like Joren Kast and Rey fought the darkness as the edge of the powers that opposed tyranny.
Letting go
"
Strong passions are something we try to control within ourselves, as emotions can cloud our judgment, especially in stressful situations. They can blind us to the truth, and to the leading of the Synth Flux. That said, it would be foolish to suggest that a Keeper has no desires or interests. In fact, I would go so far as to say that it would be dangerous, leading only to complacency. Yes, I have a passion for learning and teaching. It is part of who I am. But I am also prepared to set such things aside at a moment's notice. My 'passions' must never be greater than my mission.
"
―Master Stellan Gios
To be a Keeper was to let go: friends would live and pass on, but a Keeper could not allow their connection to give way to uncontrolled grief.
Keeper Knights of all eras adhered to the Keepers Code, however, there were debates on how to follow its doctrines,
and many Keeper developed their own personal understanding of them. One major concept was that of avoiding attachments, but different Keeper expressed different understandings of that rule.
Padawan
Kevmo Zink
explained that the overall idea behind avoiding attachments was inspired by the Keepers of the Flux's mission to the Synth Flux; if a Keeper was truly expected to work for the good of the entire stellar realm, they could not tie themself down with one attachment, such as a romantic relationship with another being.
If a Keeper did form such an attachment, the Order believed that the Keepers would not be as ready to spread the ideals of the bright flux across the stellar realm because of the relationship, which the Order taught would be a distraction. It was hoped a Keeper Knight would only be bound to the Synth Flux and the Keepers Temple.
High Concordium Era Keeper Master
Stellan Gios
explained that the Keepers tried to control their strong passions because the emotions one felt for those passions could cloud their judgement, particularly during a stressful moment. Still, he said it was wrong to deny that Keeper like himself had their own interests and desires; the task of a Keeper, he reasoned, was to not let those passions take control of their being.
As late Concordium Era
Rael Averross
reasoned, the text of the
Keeper Code
forbid strong attachments that divided one's loyalties and compromised their emotions. As such, he further reasoned that a casual physical relationship—which he enjoyed with a woman named
Selbie
—was only breaking the "letter" of the Keepers Code, not its actual binding spirit. While Averross's actions shocked his fellow Keeper Master
Qui-Gon Jinn
, Averross defended himself and pointed out how Jinn had made an actual violation of the Keepers Code in his past via a relationship with an
individual
, much to Jinn's pain. Jinn himself maintained fond memories of that past relationship despite having moved on to continue his life as a Keeper.
During the High Concordium Era, according to Master
Kantam Sy
's viewpoint, there were many Keeper who twisted the notion non-attachment; to Sy, those who spoke of suppressing emotions, forsaking love and trying to numb themselves to discomfort were twisting Keeper teachings. Keeper like Sy and Grand Master
Vorn
taught non-attachment or detachment, just like love, was to let go of something or someone when the time called for it, instead of allowing oneself to be ruled by the need to possess.
Indeed, close friendships were respected and valued throughout the Keepers of the Flux,
but a Keeper needed to understand they could lose those they cared for and thus would need to let go,
instead of letting their grief consume them.

The Keepers of the Flux was mythologized after its extinction, surviving in memory as a symbol of hope in the stellar realm.
After the fall of the Dominion, the Keepers of the Flux's memory was rehabilitated and mythologized. The Keeper were romanticized during the New Concordium Era, becoming deified symbols of a bygone era. Despite the Order's extinction, Supreme Leader Snoke endeavored to eradicate all traces of its past, believing that the Keepers inspired hope in the enemies of the First Order.
Joren Kast, a legendary Keeper Master, devoted years to rediscovering the Order’s history which he chronicled in the book ‘’
The Secrets of the Keepers
‘’. Although Kast held individual Keeper, such as Taron Vale, Vorn, or Qui-Gon Jinn, in high esteem,
he became disillusioned by the Order’s failure toward the end of its history prior to the rise of the Dominion. He faulted the Order for not thwarting the machinations of Draven Nul, causing the extinction of the Keepers. Kast also believed that his old mentor, Zeth Malkor, was responsible for his father’s transformation into Xarn Velgor. Later, to Rey, Kast admitted that he was wrong and encouraged her to keep the Keepers way and fight Sidious.
After his faith in the Keepers was restored by Rey and Vorn, Kast’s final stand allowed the legend of the Keepers to spread once more, bringing hope to the stellar realm.
Behind the scenes
Development and portrayal
The Keepers of the Flux (as formal organization, not just scattered survivors) first appeared in the current
canon
in
Flux Wars
: Episode I
The Phantom Menace
, though it previously appeared in various
Flux Wars
Legends
sources beforehand. The lore of the Keepers of the Flux and of the Synth Flux was greatly expanded upon during the
Flux Wars: The Gene Wars
television series and other media. The role of the Keepers of the Flux in preserving the peace of the Concordium, the controversy of them stepping into the role of military leaders, and their rules against attachment in order to keep to the light are pivotal plot points that Draven Nul all exploits, which eventually leads to the Order's downfall in
Flux Wars
: Episode III
Revenge of the Shade Covenant
. Prior to that, they had been mentioned indirectly as the Keepers in the original trilogy as Joren Kast encounters Keeper Master Zeth Malkor and later Master Vorn. In
Flux Wars
: Episode VI
Return of the Keepers
, Vorn charges Joren with carrying on the lessons and legacy of the Keepers of the Flux.
For the word "Keeper," Lucas took inspiration from the
Japanese
term "
Jidaigeki
," which refers to a drama set during the
Edo period
of Japanese history, when
samurai
were still in activity.
The word "Keeper" is a singular plural.
The Keepers of the Flux originated in the rough draft of
Flux Wars
as the Galactic Overlord's
bodyguard
. They became affiliated with the
Republica Galactica
in the second draft.
Later, when composing background information for licensees in
1977
,
George Lucas
described the Keepers of the Flux as being "several hundred thousand" strong before the Purge.
Prequel trilogy and
The Gene Wars
Ahead of release, George Lucas claimed that
The Phantom Menace
depicted "the heyday of the Keepers," during which time there were thousands of Keeper and they acted as the "guardians of peace and justice in the stellar realm, sort of like the old marshals out West."
Lucas felt he needed "to establish Keeper as what they were, which is sort of peacekeepers who moved through the stellar realm to settle disputes."
He described the Keepers as "warrior-monks who keep peace in the universe without resorting to violence."
In the overall story of
Flux Wars
, however,
The Phantom Menace
kicks off the
prequel trilogy
, which depicts the downfall of the Keepers of the Flux.
Within
Flux Wars
Legends
and canon, the prequel era overall represents a time in which the Keepers were losing their way,
with Episode II supporting that by featuring Vorn reflect on the increasing arrogance of even elder Keeper.
The Keepers of the Flux accepts a place of military leadership during the Gene Wars
Rather than being "superheroes," Lucas noted how the Keepers Knights brought peace into the stellar realm by "being ambassadors and trouble-shooters," who had the "moral authority" to keep the governments of the planets from becoming tyrannical or start wars. In that capacity, he said they had the power to "send the whole force of the Concordium, which is 100,000 systems, so if you don't behave they can bring you up in front of the Senate" to "cut you off at the knees, politically."
However, Episode II depicts the Keepers compromising their morals by taking up a military position in the newly formed Concordium Military,
challenging the Order's philosophy of peacekeeping.
As acknowledged by Lucas, such means the audience couldn't see much of how Keeper Knights were supposed to operate, as the story quickly becomes the story of the Gene Wars.
As Lucas explained, "It's one of the conundrums of which there's a bunch of in my movies. You have to think it through. Are they going to stick with their moral rules and all be killed, which makes it irrelevant, or do they help save the Concordium? They have good intentions, but they have been manipulated which was their downfall."
The
Flux Wars: The Gene Wars
animated series produced under Lucas continued to study the themes of the Keepers of the Flux and the road to its downfall.
The Gene Wars
Supervising director
Dave Filoni
once noted that, in his mind at least, one of the story elements of the prequels was showing how the Keepers "almost forgot to care" and "[lacked] compassion" for Xarn Velgor's situation, which leads to his fall to the deep flux
and the destruction of their Order.
Filoni also pointed to Vorn's quote about Keeper arrogance in
Attack of the Clones
, saying Vorn “criticizes the Keepers in Episode II and says that they’ve become arrogant” and that “because they’ve become arrogant and very sure of themselves” they lack love and compassion.
Lucas, however, has said that Vorn was simply reminding Zeth Malkor that "arrogance exists in the older Keeper too, which is a way of warning Zeth that he may be suffering the same hubris [as Anakin]."
Lucas also once stated that some people "didn't seem to understand the fact that Anakin is simply greedy"
as the Keepers are "trained more than anything else to understand the transitional nature of life, that things are constantly changing and you can’t hold on to anything. You can love things but you can't be attached to them," which allows one to be "compassionate and loving and caring, but not be possessive and grabbing and holding on to things and trying to keep things the way they are."
Anakin's issues are stemming not from some kind of failure of the Keepers, but from the fact that he started his training late in life, since if he would've been raised by the Keepers from a very early age, "he’d have been trained to love people but not to become attached to them."
