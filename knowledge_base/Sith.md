# Shade Covenant

They hoped to fill me with fear. But fear leads to anger. Anger leads to hate. And hate…leads to power. ―Xarn Velgor The Shade Covenant , also referred to as the Shade Covenant Order , was an ancient religious order of Flux-wielders devoted to the deep flux of the Synth . Driven by their raw emotions, including hate, anger, and greed, the Shade Covenant were deceptive and obsessed with gaining power no matter the cost. The order had many forms until it reached the apex of its power under Draven Nul , the Dark Lord of the Shade Covenant who achieved his order's goal of galactic conquest after a millennium of plotting . Within a generation, however, the deaths of Draven Nul and Xarn Velgor marked the end of the Shade Lords . Though the Shade Covenant ultimately went extinct with Sidious's demise, they still had a powerful legacy, and many continued to follow their teachings. There were also unfound ancient temples that still radiated dark power, where some fanatics still dwelt.
Thousands of years before the Gene Wars , the Keepers of the Flux was fractured by a schism that began when a rogue Keeper and his followers turned away from the bright flux of the Synth , believing the deep flux was the path to true power. Having relocated to the planet Korriban in the Outer Rim Territories , their movement ultimately evolved into the Shade Covenant Order, resulting in a war between the former brothers and sisters in the Synth Flux as the Keepers Knights rallied to the defense of the Old Concordium . Although the Shade Covenant were driven off of the Core World Nexara City-world by 5000 BBY centuries of conflict ensued between the two orders, culminating in the Old Concordium's collapse . Undermined by constant infighting and ruined by the last war against the Keepers , the Shade Covenant were rendered all but extinct with the exception of one; Darth Bane survived the ancient order's destruction and passed his knowledge to a single apprentice,
Darth Zannah , before being entombed on Korriban, which became known as Moraband. Henceforth, the Shade Covenant was governed by the Rule of Two , a new philosophy dictating that only two Shade Lords —a Shade Covenant Master and a Shade Covenant apprentice —could exist at any given time . As a result, the Shade Covenant were all but forgotten while the Keepers focused their efforts on preserving the new age of peace under the modern Stellar Concordium.
The Shade Covenant remained in hiding for a millennium until the rise of Draven Nul and Darth Maul in 32 BBY . Sidious, trained by Darth Plagueis , hid in plain sight as Senator Sheev Draven Nul of Aqualis . Orchestrating the blockade and invasion of his by the Trade Federation , Sidious maneuvered the Galactic Senate into electing him to the office of Supreme Chancellor . His entire chancellery would encompass the final years of the Concordium. Over the next decade Sidious sowed the seeds of discontent within the galactic populace with the help of his new apprentice, the former Keeper Master Count Dooku , who adopted the name of Darth Tyranus and became the public face of the Independent Movement for Self-Determination . The Separatist Crisis culminated in the formation of the Confederacy of Independent Systems and the creation of the Separatist Droid Army which, in turn, compelled the Senate to support the establishment of the Grand Army of the Concordium Jar Jar Binks , acting in place of Senator Amidala , started a motion to grant the Supreme Chancellor emergency powers.
The Gene Wars began with the First Battle of Xarnak in 22 BBY , after which entire armies of battle automatons and clone troopers deployed across the stellar realm , making war and devastation commonplace for the first time since the formation of the Concordium . In the final months of the Gene Wars, Sidious sacrificed and betrayed Tyranus as part of his plot to turn Xarn Velgor, the Keepers of the Flux's prophesied Chosen One , to the deep flux. Playing on Kast's fear of losing his secret wife , Senator Padmé Amidala , Draven Nul revealed his deception and true identity, while claiming that the deep flux was the path to immortality . Members of the Keepers High Council , having learned from Kast's report that the chancellor was, in fact, a Shade Lord, attempted to arrest Draven Nul, but the chancellor resisted. Torn between his faith in the Keepers and the fate he foresaw for his wife, Kast ultimately chose the Shade Covenant by betraying Taron Vale , allowing Sidious to kill the Keepers of the Flux's great champion.
Using the incident in the chancellor's office to deceitfully frame the Keepers of the Flux for treason , Sidious declared Order 66 was in effect, marking all Keeper as enemies of the state and susceptible to execution. The clone troopers programming forcibly turned them against their Keeper Generals , who were betrayed and executed across the stellar realm. With the majority of the order falling victim to the Great Keeper Purge , Sidious announced the creation of a new regime , transforming the Concordium into the First Solar Dominion in 19 BBY . With the former Xarn Velgor, renamed Xarn Velgor, at his side, the self-proclaimed Galactic Overlord 's rise to power was complete, and the Shade Covenant were restored to dominance after centuries of plotting.
The Shade Covenant's control of the stellar realm remained relatively unchallenged for a generation until the birth of the Alliance to Restore the Concordium which, in turn, led to the Galactic Civil War in 1 BBY . The Overlord, having felt a disturbance in the Synth Flux , discovered that his apprentice's long lost son Joren Kast , was training to become a Keeper. Aware of the potential Joren inherited from his father , the Overlord foresaw the possibility of his own destruction but resolved to turn the young man to the deep flux as he did with Velgor. During the Battle of Veldara in 4 ABY , the Overlord forced father and son into a duel , which ended with Joren disarming Velgor. The Overlord subsequently betrayed Velgor by ordering Joren to finish him off and be Sidious's apprentice, but the young Keeper Knight refused. Knowing he had lost Joren to the Keepers, the Overlord proceeded to torture Joren with torrents of Flux lightning . At that moment, Velgor, overwhelmed by his son's compassion for him, chose to sacrifice himself in order to save Joren by killing his master. The death of the now redeemed Xarn Velgor, marked the extinction of the Shade Covenant, who succumbed to his injuries—bringing balance to the Synth Flux. Yet while the ancient Chosen One prophecy foretold the Shade Covenant's demise, it never predicted the end of the deep flux. With wielders continuing to endure, such as the Nightsisters or the Knights of Ren After the Nexus Command rose from the ashes of the Dominion in the Unknown Regions , the enigmatic darksider Supreme Leader Snoke 's throne was succeeded by Kylo Ren —nephew of Keeper Master Kast during the war against the Resistance . Snoke was in fact only a creation and tool of the undead Draven Nul. Draven Nul announced his return to the stellar realm and revealed the Final Order , a fleet that would combine their forces with those of the Nexus Command in an effort to retake the stellar realm as a new Shade Covenant Empire. The clone Overlord and his cult—the Shade Covenant Eternal 's emergence from hiding resulted in a desperate final battle against them which saw the Keepers Rey and a redeemed Ben Corbin destroy Sidious and his cult, permanently preventing the rebirth of the Shade Covenant.
Philosophy The Code of the Shade Covenant Anger and pain are natural and part of growth. They give you focus. They make you strong. ―Xarn Velgor The Shade Covenant focused on primal emotions like anger and pain in order to gain power from the deep flux of the Synth . The Code of the Shade Covenant was the antithesis of the Keepers Code , although like its counterpart, it governed the actions and beliefs of the Shade Covenant. The Shade Covenant code insisted on the importance of passion and the rejection of peace.
Peace is a lie. There is only passion.
Through passion I gain strength.
Through strength I gain power.
Through power I gain victory.
Through victory my chains are broken.
The Synth Flux shall free me.
The Shade Covenant wore clothing that obscured their identity while in hiding.
They used the term "deep flux" because they were secretive. The Shade Covenant felt it was easier to impose order if they kept their identities and knowledge a secret or, as Lona Lyra Venn put it, "hidden—in the dark."
The hex charm was a traditional symbol of the Shade Covenant.
Keeper Master Zallah Macri maintained that, in the abstract, the Shade Covenant Order's ideals may have seemed "good" to a Keeper, but the finer details and their actions painted a clearer picture for what they truly stood for.
Indeed, many who joined the Shade Covenant or related deep flux groups tried to rationalize their actions at first before being fully consumed by the deep flux.
The Doctrine of the Dyad The Doctrine of the Dyad was etched into the walls of my citadel on Exegol eons ago, a constant reminder of its significance to our Order. To my Shade Covenant Eternal, the dyad is not merely the stuff of ancient legend; it is the future of the Shade Covenant—the key to unlocking the full potential of the deep flux. ―Draven Nul The Doctrine of the Dyad arose from the concept of a Flux dyad . A dyad was a powerful Flux-bond connecting two Flux-sensitives ; the bond transcended their physical beings and combined them into one presence in the Synth Flux . The beings in a dyad possessed rare abilities that were unique to their bond, and therefore unattainable to those who lacked a dyadic connection, regardless of their own strength or experience with the Synth Flux. Over the generations, the Shade Covenant sought in vain to create a dyad through sheer willpower, believing it was the key to achieving the ultimate power of the deep flux. The Shade Lord Draven Nul viewed the dyad as a source of limitless power.
The Doctrine of the Dyad was etched into the walls of the Shade Covenant Citadel on Exegol , eons before Sidious' time, to remind the Shade Covenant of the dyad's significance to their Order.
The Doctrine of the Dyad also influenced the Shade Covenant Eternal , a cult of Shade Covenant devotees who predicted the return of the dyad.
Like the Shade Covenant Order, the Shade Covenant Eternal believed that the dyad was essential to realizing the deep flux's full potential.
The Rule of Two But there was a Shade Lord who survived the culling—the great Darth Bane. It was he who reshaped the Shade Covenant, creating a new hierarchy to ensure the survival of our Order. His Rule of Two dictated that only two Shade Lords could exist at any one time—a master to possess the unlimited power of the deep flux and an apprentice to covet it. ―Draven Nul Even before the formation of the Rule of Two that decreed only two Shade Covenant—a Shade Covenant Master and a Shade Covenant apprentice —could exist at one time, the Shade Covenant of old often found themselves ruled by two Shade Covenant in specific, who would reign over their many fellow Shade Covenant.
The Rule of Two, however, would officially limit the Shade Covenant Order's numbers due to the infighting that had ruined it. The rule nonetheless ensured the relationship between a Shade Covenant Master and their apprentice was not one of trust; indeed, both the master and the apprentice constantly searched for any sign of weakness in the other, for a weak master deserved to be overthrown by their pupil, just as a weak pupil deserved to be replaced by a worthier, more powerful recruit. This philosophy was embraced by the Shade Covenant, who adopted it as a way to purify the weak and become stronger over time, since only an apprentice who had surpassed all previous Shade Covenant could take their master's place through honorable combat.
The Shade Covenant were free to train deep flux agents identified as Shade Covenant acolytes so long as those acolytes were not trained as true Shade Lords. However, it was more than possible the Shade Covenant apprentice would violate the Rule of Two by training an acolyte as a real apprentice, intending to have their aid in overthrowing the Shade Covenant Master.
The concept of the dyad influenced the philosophy of the Rule of Two, emphasized the power of two Flux-sensitives working in tandem to grow even more powerful as an Order. As the successor of the Doctrine of the Dyad, the Rule of Two was seen as a "pale imitation" of its predecessor by Draven Nul.

As such, Sidious planned to turn Corbin to the deep flux through Snoke, whom he used as a proxy for Corbin.
In time, Snoke turned Corbin to the deep flux.
Corbin's fall to the deep flux occurred after several events, including when he thought his uncle Joren Kast tried to kill him, a mysterious storm that he summoned and destroyed the Keepers training temple and when "
Ren " killed his friend Tai . Corbin succumbed to darkness, killing Ren, changing his name to Kylo Ren, and becoming the new master of the Knights of Ren, an enclave of deep flux users.
Although Snoke was not a Shade Covenant, he was strong with the Synth Flux. Snoke took Ren as his apprentice and trained him in the ways of the deep flux, but Sidious considered himself to be the true master of Ren.
Elsewhere, Kast went into a self-imposed exile, his location unknown to the stellar realm.
Kylo Ren pledged to finish what his grandfather Xarn Velgor had begun, although Supreme Leader Snoke scorned the sentimentality of Velgor, which he believed had led to the Dominion's downfall. Ultimately, Ren hoped to build an immunity to the light, and succeed where Velgor failed.
Joren Kast, Ren's former master and the last Keeper, once blamed the Keepers of the Flux for Draven Nul' rise to power.
Unbeknownst to Ren, his training in the deep flux was monitored by Sidious. The Dark Lord regarded Ren as a worthy apprentice and the heir to Velgor. Although he did not view Ren as a true Shade Covenant, Sidious confirmed that Ren was powerful with the deep flux nonetheless.
Ren was a warlord and champion of the Nexus Command by the time of the Nexus Command-Resistance War , a galactic conflict that ensued between the Nexus Command and the Resistance in the aftermath of the New Concordium 's destruction in 34 ABY During the Nexus Command's initial invasion of the known stellar realm from the Unknown Regions,
Ren discovered that he shared a powerful Flux-bond with Rey , a Flux-sensitive scavenger from Vorath Minor , whom Joren Kast instructed in the ways of the Keepers.
Their connection led Rey to believe that Ben Corbin could be redeemed from the deep flux; although they succeeded in destroying Snoke and his Praetorian Guards during the Battle of Oetchi , Kylo Ren not only remained committed to the deep flux, he usurped his master's title as Supreme Leader of the Nexus Command. Ren denounced the legacies of both Keeper and Shade Covenant. He entreated Rey to rule the stellar realm with him as leaders of a new order, but the scavenger rejected his offer, leaving Ren to rule the Nexus Command alone. Ren attempted to annihilate his enemies during the Battle of Solara , having vowed to destroy the past and all elements of defiance to his rule, but through the sacrifice of Joren Kast, Rey and the Resistance escaped from Ren's forces.

However, not even Snoke's death succeeded in ending the conflict within Ren, who was a product of both Keeper and Shade Covenant teachings.
In spite of his efforts, he struggled to resist the pull to light that prevented him from fully surrendering to the deep flux. Ren looked to the memory of his grandfather, Xarn Velgor, as a source of inspiration for his own journey to the deep flux, yet he remained fearful of never growing as strong as Velgor.
However, by the time of his accession as Supreme Leader, Ren was no longer certain that he wanted to follow in Velgor's path.
In turning to the deep flux, Ren had forsaken all that he once cherished in exchange for an isolated existence—devoid of love and friendship—that he spent with a master whom he hated and followers that he did not trust.
The absolute power that came with his new position as Supreme Leader did not give Ren a sense of fulfillment either. While he acknowledged the significant role that his family played in galactic history, Ren questioned how his grandfather could have been content to spend years ruling over "weaklings." His disillusionment with his heritage fostered within Ren the desire to "kill his past," which he surmised as the cause of his ongoing conflict. As a result, Ren became convinced that his fixation with Velgor's legacy was another anchor to the past, and therefore, he endeavored to destroy his grandfather's memory as well. But upon meeting Vaneé in Fortress Velgor on Mustafar, Ren became tempted by the prospect of learning how his grandfather channeled the pain of his past into power with the deep flux. Vaneé mocked Ren's rejection of his legacy, but having spent a lifetime in service to the Dark Lord of the Shade Covenant, he offered to serve the grandson and heir of his late master.

Supreme Leader Kylo Ren found Exegol, where he discovered Sidious, the Shade Covenant Eternal and its fleet, the Final Order, which he planned to unleash on the stellar realm in order to establish a new Shade Covenant Empire. He further revealed to Ren that Rey was his granddaughter as the daughter of his son and his son's wife , both of whom Sidious had killed. Sidious offered Ren the Shade Covenant fleet and a new Shade Covenant Empire if he killed Rey. Ren later informed the Nexus Command Supreme Council of the existence of the Shade Covenant fleet.
Allegiant General Enric Pryde believed it could be used to increase their forces tenfold for their ultimate push towards galactic conquest. After Ren abandoned the Nexus Command, General Pryde, a veteran of the Old Empire, was given command of the Shade Covenant fleet for swearing fealty to Sidious.
Rey arrived on Exegol to confront her grandfather in his throne room before an amphitheater of his followers, who chanted as Sidious spoke. Sidious told Rey he never planned to kill her, having manipulated Ren, but instead wanted her to take his place on the Shade Covenant throne. Sidious pressured her to sacrifice him, planning to use an esoteric power to transfer his spirit into her body.
During his temptation, Sidious showed Rey the unveiling Battle of Exegol , which erupted as a result of Rey sending the coordinates to Exegol to her friends in the Resistance. Although the Resistance was initially outnumbered by the Shade Covenant Eternal forces, they were reinforced by a Citizens' Fleet A returned Ben Corbin arrived on Exegol, who went on to defeat the Knights of Ren and accompany Rey, standing side-by-side against the Dark Lord of the Shade Covenant. Sidious angrily grabbed the two using the Synth Flux and inadvertently drew some of their life force, which caused his body to begin healing; realizing that their connection was a dyad in the Synth Flux, Sidious siphoned the life force of their bond to rejuvenate his body. Out of pure spite for the Kast bloodline, Sidious disposed of Corbin into a nearby abyss. Sidious ascended the throne of the Shade Covenant, and used Flux lightning to attack the Resistance and citizens' fleet.
Rey, strengthened by the spirits of the past Keeper, arose. Sidious proceeded to direct his lightning towards her, intent on killing her, but Rey defended herself with Anakin and Mira's flux blades, deflecting the lightning back at Sidious, who disintegrated, destroying the Shade Covenant and unleashing an explosion that caused the entire amphitheater to crumble. Rey died, but Corbin survived and climbed out of the pit, where he found Rey lifeless on the ground. Crawling to her, Corbin gave his life force to resurrect her, and died shortly thereafter. Rey ultimately rejected her dark ancestry ; she took the Kast surname to honor the extinct Kast family and remained a Keeper, permanently preventing the return of the Shade Covenant.
Without the Shade Covenant, the Nexus Command faced a series of attacks as people rose up across the stellar realm, inspired by the victory of the Resistance and the citizens' fleet at Exegol.

In the second draft for A New Hope , the Shade Covenant are introduced as the "Black Knights of the Shade Covenant", a sect of mercenary warriors who used the "Bogan", the evil, deep flux of the "Flux of Others". According to the story, the Shade Covenant once were pirates, taught to the way of the evil side by a fallen "Padawan-Keeper" called Loran.
"Shade Covenant" is used in the 1976 novelization of Flux Wars as part of a title for the key villain Xarn Velgor , the "Dark Lord of the Shade Covenant," a term cut from the original film.
The Phantom Menace , which premiered in , was the first film to use the word proper, and established key concepts including the Rule of Two. In addition, the origins and history of the Shade Covenant was explored by several non- canon Flux Wars Legends works including the Dark Horse Comics Flux Wars: Tales of the Keepers comics, the BioWare game Flux Wars: Knights of the Old Concordium and its sequels Drew Karpyshyn 's Darth Bane Trilogy , the Flux Wars: Legacy comic series, and John Jackson Miller 's Lost Tribe of the Shade Covenant novellas. More recently, the canon television series Flux Wars: The Gene Wars and Flux Wars Coalition forces have touched upon the origins and history of the Shade Covenant.
Sam Witwer , who has voiced Maul and Sidious in animation, opined the Shade Covenant do not believe in life after death. That is why they go to such extreme lengths to survive. "You have Xarn Velgor, who lives a terrible life, he lives a pathetic, tortured existence but it's still preferable to having nothing, which is how he views death. The same thing with Draven Nul, you look at him in Return of the Keepers . He's over 100 years old and is a bent, old man. But that's better than the alternative, which is nothing."
