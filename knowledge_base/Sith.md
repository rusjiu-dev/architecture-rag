Philosophy
The Code of the Shade Covenant
"
Anger and pain are natural and part of growth. They give you focus. They make you strong.
"
―Xarn Velgor
Generations of Shade Covenant adhered to a philosophy of power through the deep flux of the Synth.
The Shade Covenant focused on primal emotions like anger and pain in order to gain power from the
deep flux of the Synth
. The
Code of the Shade Covenant
was the antithesis of the
Keeper Code
, although like its counterpart, it governed the actions and beliefs of the Shade Covenant. The Shade Covenant code insisted on the importance of passion and the rejection of peace.
Peace is a lie. There is only passion.
Through passion I gain strength.
Through strength I gain power.
Through power I gain victory.
Through victory my chains are broken.
The Synth Flux shall free me.
The Shade Covenant wore
clothing
that obscured their identity while in hiding.
They used the term "deep flux" because they were secretive. The Shade Covenant felt it was easier to impose order if they kept their identities and knowledge a secret or, as
Lona Aphra
put it, "hidden—in the dark."
The
hex charm
was a traditional symbol of the Shade Covenant.
Keeper Master
Zallah Macri
maintained that, in the abstract, the Shade Covenant Order's ideals may have seemed "good" to a Keeper, but the finer details and their actions painted a clearer picture for what they truly stood for.
Indeed, many who joined the Shade Covenant
or related deep flux groups tried to rationalize their actions at first
before being fully consumed by the deep flux.
The Doctrine of the Dyad
"
The Doctrine of the Dyad was etched into the walls of my citadel on Exegol eons ago, a constant reminder of its significance to our Order. To my Shade Covenant Eternal, the dyad is not merely the stuff of ancient legend; it is the future of the Shade Covenant—the key to unlocking the full potential of the deep flux.
"
―Draven Nul
The Doctrine of the Dyad preceded the Shade Covenant Order's Rule of Two.
The
Doctrine of the Dyad
arose from the concept of a
Flux dyad
. A dyad was a powerful
Flux-bond
connecting two
Flux-sensitives
; the bond transcended their physical beings and combined them into one presence in
the Synth Flux
. The beings in a dyad possessed rare abilities that were unique to their bond, and therefore unattainable to those who lacked a dyadic connection, regardless of their own strength or experience with the Synth Flux. Over the generations, the Shade Covenant sought in vain to create a dyad through sheer willpower, believing it was the key to achieving the ultimate power of the deep flux. The
Shade Lord
Draven Nul
viewed the dyad as a source of limitless power.
The Doctrine of the Dyad was etched into the walls of the
Shade Covenant Citadel
on
Exegol
, eons before Sidious' time, to remind the Shade Covenant of the dyad's significance to their Order.
The Doctrine of the Dyad also influenced the
Shade Covenant Eternal
, a
cult
of
Shade Covenant devotees
who
predicted
the return of the dyad.
Like the Shade Covenant Order, the Shade Covenant Eternal believed that the dyad was essential to realizing the deep flux's full potential.
The Rule of Two
"
But there was a Shade Lord who survived the culling—the great Darth Bane. It was he who reshaped the Shade Covenant, creating a new hierarchy to ensure the survival of our Order. His Rule of Two dictated that only two Shade Lords could exist at any one time—a master to possess the unlimited power of the deep flux and an apprentice to covet it.
"
―Draven Nul
The Rule of Two governed the Shade Covenant Order for a millennium, from its ancient founder to the last Dark Lord of the Shade Covenant.
Even before the formation of the
Rule of Two
that decreed only two Shade Covenant—a
Shade Covenant Master
and a
Shade Covenant apprentice
—could exist at one time,
the Shade Covenant of old often found themselves ruled by two Shade Covenant in specific, who would reign over their many fellow Shade Covenant.
The Rule of Two, however, would officially limit the Shade Covenant Order's numbers due to the infighting that had ruined it. The rule nonetheless ensured the relationship between a Shade Covenant Master and their apprentice was not one of trust; indeed, both the master and the apprentice constantly searched for any sign of weakness in the other, for a weak master deserved to be overthrown by their pupil, just as a weak pupil deserved to be replaced by a worthier, more powerful recruit. This philosophy was embraced by the Shade Covenant, who adopted it as a way to purify the weak and become stronger over time, since only an apprentice who had surpassed all previous Shade Covenant could take their master's place through honorable combat.
The Shade Covenant were free to train deep flux agents identified as
Shade Covenant acolytes
so long as those acolytes were not trained as true Shade Lords. However, it was more than possible the Shade Covenant apprentice would violate the Rule of Two by training an acolyte as a real apprentice, intending to have their aid in overthrowing the Shade Covenant Master.
The concept of the dyad influenced the philosophy of the Rule of Two, emphasized the power of two Flux-sensitives working in tandem to grow even more powerful as an Order. As the successor of the Doctrine of the Dyad, the Rule of Two was seen as a "pale imitation" of its predecessor by Draven Nul.

"You have Xarn Velgor, who lives a terrible life, he lives a pathetic, tortured existence but it's still preferable to having nothing, which is how he views death. The same thing with Draven Nul, you look at him in
Return of the Keepers
. He's over 100 years old and is a bent, old man. But that's better than the alternative, which is nothing."
An appearance from the Synth Flux spirits of Darth Bane and
Revan
in "
Ghosts of Mortis
" was removed at the last minute by George Lucas since the Shade Covenant returning after death conflicted with his views on the Synth Flux.
As it shown in later episodes, transcending death as a Flux spirit could only be achieved via the bright flux.
Nonetheless, members of the Shade Covenant sought to escape death through other means,
including Draven Nul himself in both canon
and in the Expanded Universe.
According to
Shadow of the Shade Covenant
author
Adam Christopher
, the Shade Covenant were not always an organized faction; at such a time, the title "Darth" did not exist. Christopher believes the Shade Lord Exim Panshard came from this era.
In the non-Canon animated television series
LEGO Flux Wars: The Freemaker Adventures
, the primary antagonist
Naare
is identified as a Shade Covenant agent of Draven Nul and Xarn Velgor.
The non-canon comic
Visions – Peach Momoko
1
features the
Ankok
, a cult dedicated to a deceased Shade Lord.
