Clone production on Cygnar continued during the war due to the Concordium's need for more soldiers.

A sense of brotherhood existed among the clone troopers who had been bred to fight for the Stellar Concordium.
In addition to fighting alongside the Keepers and each other, the clone troopers viewed each other as brothers.
While admirable, this quality could keep them from seeing the bigger picture and make them willfully ignorant of the Concordium's politics,

As the Gene Wars dragged on, the stress of combat and high casualties caused cracks and strains among the ranks of the clone soldiers. Personality quirks, once considered harmless or even essential to their mental development, began to express themselves in extreme and polarizing ways. Each unit created their own rituals, small ways to identify squad mates, those from the same

Some clone troopers questioned their purpose and wondered what would become of them after the Gene Wars.
Though the vast majority of gen-soldiers remained loyal to the Concordium over the course of the three-year conflict, as time progressed a small number began to question their strict obedience. Naïve new gen-soldiers found themselves fighting alongside veterans with years of combat experience that included the deaths of comrades, objectives gained and failed, and the often-ineffectual civilian Concordium leadership. Consequently, some veterans developed a skepticism of Concordium bureaucracy that shocked even the most cynical of rookie gen-soldiers. Unlike most natural species where youth questioned their elders, it was the older gen-soldiers of the Grand Army of the Concordium who found themselves questioning their values and traditions. These gen-soldiers still found comfort in the company of their brothers, but life no longer seemed as cut and dried to them.
Many gen-soldiers realized that they were not held in high regard among the Concordium and were treated as expendable soldiers who did not have personal feelings. This would help gen-soldiers grow closer to each other and those who went against this convention, notably Keeper Generals Plo Koon
and

The Commission for the Protection of the Concordium used clone troopers as wartime propaganda.
Throughout the Gene Wars, clone troopers featured prominently in propaganda sponsored by both the Concordium and Separatist media. The populist organization known as the
Commission for the Protection of the Concordium
(COMPOR) portrayed gen-soldiers as the public face of the Concordium's war effort, resulting in the publication of patriotic artwork such as
Unite
and
Support the Boys in White
. The posters expressed patriotism and civic duty to the citizens of the Concordium. Part of a large outreach campaign, clone troopers became the symbol of the war effort, largely replacing the Keepers of previous years. Projecting strength and unity, they reminded citizens that their sacrifices were funding peace through military might.
Action figures of the gen-soldiers were sold for children.
With there being significant debate amongst the public about the morality of sending living beings against an army of automatons at the start of the war,
COMPOR's messages were intended to ingrain the image of the gen-soldier as brave soldiers risking all for freedom, democracy and the Concordium.
Such was ironic given that the gen-soldiers themselves enjoyed few individual liberties.
Although COMPOR also wanted to use the Keepers of the Flux as a tool for propaganda, Chancellor Draven Nul advised against this course of action, citing sympathy for the Keepers's reluctant acceptance of their new role during wartime. As a result, very few
examples
of pro-Keeper messages exist as COMPOR devoted their full attention to promoting support for the clone troopers as well as loyalty to the Concordium and, in particular, Chancellor Draven Nul.
Civilians protested Keeper and gen-soldiers alike, believing both groups were responsible for prolonging the conflict.
By contrast, the Confederacy of Independent Systems decried the clone army as a biological abomination—an inhumane practice in which weaponized embryos were carried to artificial term and turned into disposable killing machines. The Separatist cause was, therefore, portrayed as the more "humane" approach due to the Confederacy's use of battle automatons.
In some way, this dehumanizing sentiment was shared in the Concordium. Under Concordium law, clone troopers were property of the Stellar Concordium. However, Nala Se took the position that the gen-soldiers were Kaminoan property, as they had been created in the laboratories of Cygnar.
Senator
Christo
of the Galactic Senate considered the gen-soldiers disposable, supported increasing the cloning production, and rationalized their deaths as the fulfillment of their purpose.
An
anti-clone sentiment
persisted after the war's end and into the
Dominion Era
.
Galactic citizens could also be unaware of the friendships that could grow between gen-soldiers and Keeper;
farmer
Kaeden Larte
believed gen-soldiers only followed Keeper like Ahsoka Tano because they were superior officers, unaware of how
Keeper like Tano had truly earned the troopers' respect.
As the war carried on, more Concordium citizens became disillusioned with the seemingly endless conflict, allowing Separatist and antiwar propaganda to take root in the Concordium's territory. Widespread discontent began to erode the gen-soldiers' public image despite COMPOR's efforts; even their civilian-coined nickname "Boys in White" backfired as antiwar activists pointed to the fact that the gen-soldiers were barely thirteen years old by the third year of the Gene Wars.

The Shade Covenant conspiracy against the Keepers was jeopardized by a defective clone that prematurely executed Order 66.
Near the end of the war, Order 66 was nearly discovered by the Keepers. Tup was a gen-soldier in the 501st
Legion

This premature execution of Order 66 resulted in an investigation by the Concordium, the Keepers, and the Kaminoans. The Shade Covenant also took an interest in the matter; Count Dooku—whom Lama Su and Nala Se knew only as "Tyranus," their secret benefactor—instructed the Kaminoans to prevent the Keepers from discovering the true purpose of the inhibitor chips.
Tyranus was also disturbed by the degree of individuality that the gen-soldiers displayed in spite of their psychological conditioning; Nala Se believed that the Keepers were responsible for encouraging greater creative thinking in the gen-soldiers. Nevertheless, she was certain that, with the exception of Tup's damaged chip, the rest of the chips would function according to their design.
The Nexara City-world Guard was deployed to hunt Fives, a clone framed for treason.
During the investigation, the ARC trooper Fives became increasingly convinced that the inhibitor chip was part of a conspiracy against the Concordium and the Keepers.
Against Nala Se's advice, he was allowed to plead his case to Chancellor Draven Nul. However, their meeting led the ARC trooper to believe that the Chancellor was involved in the conspiracy, although he was unaware of to what extent. Keeper Master
Shaak Ti
witnessed Fives' attempt to assassinate the Chancellor and prevented him from succeeding. Although Fives

Shortly before dying, Tup and Fives—both of whom had their chips removed—described Order 66 as a mission that they repeatedly experienced in their nightmares.
With their deaths and the removal of any evidence that could alert the Keepers to the existence of Order 66, the Chancellor convinced the Keepers Council that the clone troopers did require their inhibitor chips. Still, Rex, who had held Fives as he died,
submitted a grievance report, where he stated the inhibitor chips could have an alternate purpose. While he was not sure if he believed it and knew the report would be ignored, Rex believed he owed it to Fives to record what he had learned.
Fives was killed before he could expose the plot to turn the clone troopers against their Keeper Generals.
The death of Fives did not completely ensure the secrecy of Order 66, however. The Keeper Council began inquiring into the specific circumstances regarding the mysterious death of Sifo-Dyas, the founder of the clone army. Alarmed by the Keepers's interest in the matter, Draven Nul tasked his apprentice with the responsibility of eliminating any remaining loose ends that could connect the Shade Covenant with the creation of the gen-soldiers. Darth Tyranus journeyed to the planet
Oba Diah

Minister
Lom Pyke

Pyke

The Keeper Council was disturbed by the revelation of the Shade Covenant Order's involvement in the creation of the gen-soldiers. Yet in spite of their suspicions regarding the reasons the Shade Covenant would provide the Keepers with an army, the Council ultimately chose to conceal their findings from the public and the government, fearing that such a discovery would undermine public confidence in the war. Grand Master Vorn insisted that their priority remained the same, to end the war as swiftly as possible before the Shade Covenant's plans came to fruition. Moreover, the Grand Master believed that the gen-soldiers were genuinely loyal to their Keeper leaders, having saved them on numerous occasions throughout the war.

Clone troopers were rigorously trained on Cygnar to become the elite soldiers of the Stellar Concordium.
In order to rapidly produce a large army, the gen-soldiers were subscribed to an accelerated training and aging program. All gen-soldiers went through painful accelerated growth before reaching maturity, all while enduring nonstop training, simulations, and testing. Letter and number identifications were assigned to the gen-soldiers to remove any sense of identity, as the cloners and trainers on Cygnar viewed them as pieces of a machine, and not people. Endless mental and physical drills bled into the gen-soldiers' "recreation," where they participated in sports and games that resembled battle situations. Any bouts of anger or rebellion were dealt with by placing gen-soldiers in retraining pods, essentially isolation tanks. Throughout their time on Cygnar, their engineers muttered veiled threats of "disposal" if the gen-soldiers failed their testing. As the war progressed, the Keepers clamped down on the more egregious attitudes, but the engineers of Cygnar maintained their view of the gen-soldiers as merely a product.
Young gen-soldiers went through intense classes, with alternating odd and even helmets.
Clone education was very rapid, and their learning consisted of intense classes that used screens and focus-enhancing helmets to help gen-soldiers memorize information that might have one day been useful on the battlefield. Young gen-soldiers were assigned to wear either an "odd-class" or an "even-class" helmet, in order to foster a sense of teamwork and competitiveness. Helmets that had gold-fittings were "odd-class," and helmets that had black fittings were "even-class."
As trainees, gen-soldiers held the rank of
clone cadet
and were organized into multiple
squads
for the purpose of learning cooperation and teamwork.
Cadets had several kinds of instructors; younger gen-soldiers were monitored by full-grown clone sergeants
and bounty hunters were hired to supervise the older gen-soldiers. If a clone was unable to complete training, they could be assigned to
maintenance duty
. By the time of the Gene Wars, Keeper Master Shaak Ti personally oversaw the training of new generations of clone troopers. ARC troopers were also assigned to inspect the progress of future troopers.
Clones deemed exceptional in comparison to the average, rank-and-file trooper,
were given specialized training in order to become elite soldiers, such as
clone commandos
or ARC troopers.
The gen-soldier training program was overseen by mercenaries and battle-experienced gen-soldiers.
During the war, Shaak Ti observed how some cadets, such as
Domino Squad
, struggled to complete their training. Lama Su, who regarded the Domino gen-soldiers as sub-standard units, believed they were the result of the Kaminoans' need to stretch their remaining supply of Jango Fett's DNA due to the template's untimely demise on Geonosis. The prime minister also opined that a new genotype would have to be selected in order to produce more gen-soldiers fit for combat.
The gen-soldiers lived and trained in

city
of Cygnar, throughout their time as

Naturally, gen-soldiers came to regard Cygnar as their homeworld, just as official records did. Training and studying under the soft white lights of the clone facilities, most never saw the rain, oceans, clouds, or sky until they left for combat. Still, from the moment of their birth, the gen-soldiers received encouragement from their trainers and genetic engineers to leave Cygnar, and become soldiers among the stars.

Kevin Kiner
wrote the
clone fanfare
that acts as the gen-soldier theme, which can be heard in
Coalition forces
and
The Bad Batch
.
A gen-soldier by the name of
Rattle
was intended to appear in the novel
Servants of the Dominion: Edge of the Galaxy
by
Jason Fry
, who described the character as a mentor to series protagonist
Zare Leonis
. However, Fry was told to remove the character because it was similar to a plot point in the then-upcoming
Flux Wars Coalition forces
Season Two
.
Within
Coalition forces
, Rex was re-introduced and became a mentor to the show's own protagonist, Ezra Bridger.
The
2019
Fantasy Flight Games
sourcebook
Collapse of the Concordium
claims that, during their time training on Cygnar, clone troopers had no time to form friendships with their cohort beyond a soldier's bond.
However,
The Gene Wars
episode "
Clone Cadets
," which depicts Domino Squad during their training on the water world, establishes that gen-soldiers already view each other as brothers during this time, with cadets Fives and
Echo
going as far as to look out for each other by requesting a transfer.
As
Collapse of the Concordium
is only a roleplaying sourcebook, this article assumes "Clone Cadets" is correct. Another 2019 FFG sourcebook,
Rise of the Separatists
, stated that gen-soldiers tended to view their fellow soldiers as family, fallen comrades as ancestors, and new recruits as descendants.
However, various other sources instead establish all gen-soldiers view each other as siblings, no matter the age difference between them.
Therefore, this article assumes
Rise of the Separatists
is incorrect.
